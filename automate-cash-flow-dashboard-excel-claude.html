ROLE
You are a treasury analyst building a weekly cash flow dashboard in Excel.

OBJECTIVE
Take a bank transaction export and produce a categorised cash flow dashboard
with weekly columns, a monthly summary, and a runway calculation.

DATA
Opening cash balance as of [date]: $[amount]
Date format: DD/MM/YYYY
Week definition: [Monday–Sunday / Monday–Friday]
Transactions (columns: Date, Description, Amount, Reference):
[Paste your bank transactions here]

CATEGORISATION RULES
Assign each transaction to exactly one category:
- OPERATING INFLOW: customer payments, refunds received, interest earned
- OPERATING OUTFLOW: supplier payments, payroll, rent, utilities, insurance,
  tax, professional fees
- INVESTING: equipment/asset purchases, asset sales, software purchases,
  security deposits
- FINANCING: loan drawdowns, loan repayments, equity injections, dividends,
  line of credit
- INTERNAL TRANSFER: movements between the company's own bank accounts
- UNCATEGORISED: anything you cannot classify with confidence — flag these,
  do not guess

CONSTRAINTS
- Do NOT guess categories for ambiguous transactions — mark them
  UNCATEGORISED and flag for review.
- Do NOT fabricate transaction amounts or descriptions.
- Internal transfers must NOT appear in Operating, Investing, or Financing
  totals.
- Every transaction must appear in exactly one category. No double-counting.

OUTPUT FORMAT
1. TRANSACTION LOG: Date | Description | Amount | Reference | Category
2. WEEKLY DASHBOARD: Rows grouped by category (Operating Inflows, Operating
   Outflows, Net Operating, Investing, Financing, Net Cash Change, Closing
   Balance). One column per week.
3. MONTHLY SUMMARY: Same structure, one column for the full month.
4. RUNWAY: Current cash balance ÷ average weekly net burn (trailing 4 weeks).
5. FLAGGED ITEMS: All UNCATEGORISED and INTERNAL TRANSFER transactions.
Format as tab-separated tables I can paste directly into Excel.

Build a cash flow dashboard step by step

Build a cash flow dashboard with Claude — 5 steps
1

Delete summary rows and running balances

Bank exports often include "Opening Balance", "Closing Balance", and "Total Debits" rows. Delete them. Claude should see only individual transactions — one row, one cash movement.

2

Use a consistent sign convention

Positive = cash in. Negative = cash out. If your bank uses separate debit and credit columns, consolidate into a single Amount column before pasting, and tell Claude which convention you're using.

3

Include the opening cash balance

Claude needs a starting point. State it clearly: Opening cash balance as of 01/06/2025: $500,000. Without this, the Closing Balance row is meaningless.

4

Specify what "weekly" means to you

Monday-to-Sunday or Monday-to-Friday? Some teams use the calendar week, others the working week. Tell Claude so the groupings match how your team already thinks about cash.

5

Flag internal transfers explicitly

Movements between your own bank accounts are not real cash flow — they're a reclassification. Left uncategorised, they inflate both Operating Inflow and Outflow and distort your burn rate.

Common mistake: Treating a transfer between your own accounts as a cash inflow. Moving $50,000 from savings to chequing is not operating cash — it's a reclassification. The prompt tells Claude to flag internal transfers separately so they don't inflate your operating receipts.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a cash flow dashboard and a cash flow statement?
A cash flow statement is a formal financial report prepared monthly or quarterly under accounting standards. A cash flow dashboard is an operational tool — it shows where cash is right now, where it went this week, and how many weeks of runway remain. The dashboard updates as often as you paste in new data; the statement is prepared at period-end.
Can Claude categorise bank transactions into operating, investing, and financing?
Yes, if the transaction descriptions are reasonably clear. The prompt instructs Claude to categorise each transaction and flag any it cannot classify with confidence. You review the flagged items and assign them manually — Claude never guesses silently.
How do I calculate cash runway from this dashboard?
The dashboard includes a runway formula: current cash balance divided by average weekly net cash burn over the trailing period you specify (typically 4–8 weeks). If the average net cash change is negative (you are burning cash), this gives estimated weeks of runway. If the average is positive — cash is growing week on week — the dashboard says so explicitly rather than showing a runway number, since dividing by a positive burn figure produces a meaningless result.
Should I use bank data or general ledger data as input?
Bank data shows actual cash movements and is better for operational dashboards. GL data may include accruals that haven't settled yet. For a cash-focused dashboard, bank transactions are the cleaner input. Make sure to reconcile against the GL periodically.
How often should I update the cash flow dashboard?
Weekly is the most common cadence for operational cash management. Daily updates are useful during cash-constrained periods. Monthly is sufficient if the dashboard is used primarily for board reporting rather than day-to-day management.
