Claude AI for KPI Reporting

Your KPI data tells you what happened — turning it into a written narrative for management is the slow part. Paste your figures and context notes into Claude and get a structured report back, so you spend your time verifying, not writing from scratch.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
Using the following KPI data and context notes, write a structured performance report for [Audience, e.g. operations management / the leadership team]: [Insert KPI Data Table and Context Notes].

Structure the report with: an Overall Summary, commentary on each key metric, and an Actions and Next Steps section.

Crucial instruction: Base every statement on the data and context provided. Do not invent figures, calculate percentages not in the data, or suggest causes for movements not mentioned in the context notes.

Include the "why" behind every notable movement, or Claude will only describe the "what." Paste into Claude.

Crucial instruction: Describe only what the data and context show. Do not suggest causes or corrective actions not mentioned in the context provided.

Step-by-step workflow

Draft a KPI report with Claude — 5 steps
1

Export and organise your KPI data

Pull the relevant figures from your operational systems and arrange them in a simple table with the current period, previous period and any targets.

2

Note the key movements and context

Write brief notes on what drove the most significant changes — system issues, staffing, demand spikes or process improvements.

3

Ask Claude to draft the report

Paste the data table and context notes and ask Claude to produce a structured narrative with a summary, metric commentary and actions section.

4

Verify every figure in the draft

Check every number, percentage and trend statement in the draft against your source data — Claude may have interpreted a movement differently from how you would describe it.

5

Share with management

Send the verified report through your normal reporting channel or paste it into your management pack.

Verify before you send: KPI reports shared with senior management or external stakeholders represent your organisation's operational performance. Never share a report containing figures or commentary you have not personally verified.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What does Claude need to write a KPI performance report?
Your KPI data table and context notes explaining the key movements. Claude writes from the data and context you provide — it does not access your operational systems or calculate new metrics.
What will be missing from Claude's commentary if I don't include context notes?
An explanation of why each KPI moved. Claude can describe what happened but not why — if you don't include the operational context behind a change, the commentary will state the number without explaining it.
What must I do before sharing a Claude-drafted KPI report with management?
Verify every figure and trend statement against your source data. Claude may have interpreted a movement differently from how you would describe it, so nothing should go out unchecked.
What's the risk of sharing an unverified KPI report?
Errors in the report can undermine trust and lead to poor operational decisions. KPI reports shared with senior management represent your organisation's operational performance, so accuracy matters.
Can Claude tell me why a metric moved without any context from me?
No. Claude cannot tell you why a KPI moved unless you tell it — always add the operational context (staffing, demand, system issues) before asking Claude to draft the commentary.
