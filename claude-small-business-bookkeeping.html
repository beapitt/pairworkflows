Why small business bookkeeping eats your time — and how Claude cuts it down

AI Bookkeeping & Accounting Prompts for Small Business

If you run a small business, you know the feeling. You spend Sunday evening going through bank transactions, trying to remember whether that $340 charge from three weeks ago was a client lunch or a tool subscription. You're not behind because you're disorganised — you're behind because bookkeeping is genuinely tedious, and it's not the reason you started the business.

Hiring a bookkeeper fixes the problem, but it costs money every month whether you're busy or not. Doing it yourself in QuickBooks or Xero means learning software you don't really want to learn. Neither option feels right for a business that's still growing.

What Claude Does — and What It Doesn't

Claude handles the repetitive part: categorizing transactions, formatting data, producing summaries, and flagging anything that looks odd. You paste in your bank export, tell it your category list, and it gives you back a clean, structured record you can paste into your accounting software or hand to your accountant.

Claude won't file your taxes, won't connect to your bank, and won't replace professional judgment on anything complex. What it will do is turn an hour of manual categorization into five minutes — and make the time you do spend with your accountant much more productive because your records are already organized.

  • Categorize a month of bank transactions in one prompt — paste the export, get a clean table back
  • Produce a basic profit and loss summary from your income and expense totals
  • Track outstanding invoices and flag overdue payments
  • Flag uncertain or ambiguous transactions for review rather than guessing
  • Format output ready to paste into Excel, Google Sheets, QuickBooks, or Xero
  • Separate personal spending from business spending on mixed accounts

Ready-to-Use Claude Prompts for Small Business Bookkeeping

Three prompts covering the most common bookkeeping tasks. Each has placeholders in [square brackets] — fill them in before you send. The more context you give Claude about your business, the more accurate the output.

Prompt 1 — Categorize a month of bank transactions

Paste your bank export into this prompt. Claude will categorize every transaction, flag anything ambiguous, and output a table ready for your spreadsheet or accounting software.

Prompt — paste into Claude
I run a [type of business — e.g. freelance design studio / e-commerce store / consulting firm] in [country]. Below are my bank transactions for [month, year]. Please categorize each one for my business bookkeeping records. Use only these categories: Income | Software & Subscriptions | Contractor Fees | Marketing | Travel | Office & Equipment | Professional Services | Bank Fees | Personal (non-business) | Uncategorized — Review Rules: - If you're confident about a category, assign it. - If a transaction could reasonably be personal or business (e.g. Amazon, a supermarket), mark it "Personal — Review" and add a note. - Do NOT guess on amounts. Output exactly what I give you. - Flag any transaction over $[threshold, e.g. 500] in the Notes column. Output a table with columns: Date | Description | Amount | Category | Notes TRANSACTIONS: [Paste your bank export rows here — date, description, amount is enough] After the table, add a category summary: total income, total by expense category, and net.

Prompt 2 — Track outstanding invoices and flag overdue

Paste your invoice list and Claude will calculate days outstanding, flag overdue invoices, and tell you your total receivables at a glance.

Prompt — paste into Claude
I need to track my outstanding invoices and identify which ones are overdue. Today's date is [today's date]. My payment terms are [e.g. Net 30 / due on receipt / 14 days]. Here are my open invoices: [Paste invoice data — one per line: Invoice #, Client name, Invoice date, Amount, Paid? (Yes/No)] For each unpaid invoice, calculate: - Days outstanding (from invoice date to today) - Status: Current / Due soon (within 7 days) / Overdue (past due date) / Seriously overdue (30+ days past due) Output a table: Invoice # | Client | Invoice Date | Due Date | Amount | Days Outstanding | Status Then add a summary: - Total outstanding - Total overdue - Clients with 2+ unpaid invoices (flag these) Do not invent invoice amounts or dates. Use only the data I've provided.

Prompt 3 — Produce a simple monthly P&L

Once your transactions are categorized, use this prompt to turn them into a formatted profit and loss statement.

Prompt — paste into Claude
I need a simple profit and loss statement for [month, year] for my [type of business] in [country]. Here are my income and expense totals for the month: INCOME: [e.g. Client revenue: $8,400] [e.g. Product sales: $1,200] EXPENSES: [e.g. Software & Subscriptions: $340] [e.g. Contractor Fees: $1,800] [e.g. Marketing: $620] [e.g. Travel: $210] [e.g. Office & Equipment: $95] [Add all expense categories] Please produce a clean P&L table with: - Total Income - Total Expenses (broken down by category) - Gross Profit - Net Profit (and net margin as a %) Format it so I can paste it into a spreadsheet or share it with my accountant. Do not add figures that aren't in the data above.
Tip: Run Prompt 1 first to categorize your transactions, then use the category totals it produces as the input for Prompt 3. The two prompts chain together naturally — you don't need to add up anything by hand.

4-Step Guide to Adapting These Prompts

The prompts above are starting points. Here's how to make them fit your specific business without rewriting them from scratch.

01

Match your category list to your accounting software

The category names in Prompt 1 are generic. Replace them with the exact account names your software uses. In QuickBooks, that might be 6000 · Advertising or 7400 · Software. In Xero, it could be Consulting & Accounting. When Claude uses your real account names, you can import the output directly without remapping anything.

02

Tell Claude which vendors are always personal

If you use a mixed account, Claude will flag anything ambiguous. Speed this up by listing your known personal vendors in the prompt: These vendors are always personal — exclude from business categories: [Spotify, Netflix, local gym]. Claude will assign those automatically instead of flagging them for review every time.

03

Add a VAT or sales tax column if you need it

For UK sole traders or VAT-registered businesses, add this line to Prompt 1: Add a VAT column. Apply 20% VAT to all business expenses unless the vendor is clearly exempt (e.g. salary, insurance, bank fees). For US businesses tracking sales tax on income, add: Flag any income line where sales tax may apply based on the product/service type. Get the rates confirmed by your accountant — don't rely on Claude for tax law.

04

Run it monthly and build a year-to-date view

Process one month at a time, paste each Claude output into a running spreadsheet, and add a summary tab that pulls year-to-date totals. By Q4 you'll have a full-year picture ready to hand to your accountant. The key is consistency — same category names, same format, every month. A one-time setup of your spreadsheet template pays off across the whole year.

Important: Claude categorizes transactions based on the description text you give it. It doesn't know your business context the way you do. Always review the output before importing it into your accounting software — especially for high-value or unusual transactions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Claude replace my bookkeeper or accountant?
No. Claude handles the repetitive data work — categorizing, formatting, summarizing — so you spend less time on admin and your accountant spends less time cleaning up your records. The professional judgment, tax advice, and filing still need a real accountant.
Which accounting software does this work with?
Any of them. Claude outputs plain tables you can paste into Excel, Google Sheets, or import into QuickBooks, Xero, Wave, or FreeAgent. Just tell Claude which category codes your software uses and it will match them.
My bank exports in CSV. Can I paste that directly?
Yes. Copy the relevant columns from your CSV — date, description, and amount are enough — and paste them into the prompt. Remove any summary rows or running balance columns first.
Can Claude produce a profit and loss statement?
Claude can produce a basic P&L from categorized transaction data you provide. It won't pull live data from your bank or accounting software, but if you paste in your income and expense totals by category, it will format and calculate the P&L clearly.
What if I mix personal and business spending on the same account?
Tell Claude in the prompt that some transactions may be personal, and give it a list of known personal vendors to flag. It will mark those as Personal rather than assigning a business category. You then review and confirm before importing.
Is this suitable for VAT or sales tax tracking?
Claude can add a VAT or tax column if you include the rate in your prompt. For mixed-rate or exempt transactions, flag them manually — getting tax rates wrong is the kind of error that costs money to fix. Always confirm with your accountant.
