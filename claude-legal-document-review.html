Step-by-step workflow

Review a legal document with Claude — 5 steps
1

Anonymise the document

Remove counterparty names, commercially sensitive figures and any information subject to confidentiality obligations. Use placeholder labels like "Party A" or "Supplier X".

2

State the context and your position

Tell Claude the contract type, jurisdiction and whether you are the buyer, seller, employer or another party — this changes what gets flagged as risky.

3

Run the extraction and risk summary

Paste the anonymised text and ask for clause-by-clause extraction with a Risk Summary at the end.

4

Verify any regulatory references

Check any statute numbers, thresholds or deadlines Claude cites against current official sources — its training data has a cutoff.

5

Send to qualified legal review

Use Claude's output as a starting point that cuts the reading time — not as a substitute for review by a qualified lawyer before signing.

Not legal advice: Claude does not provide legal advice. It summarises, extracts and drafts — it does not advise on legal risk or strategy. Any contract that will be signed or relied upon must be reviewed by a qualified lawyer in the relevant jurisdiction.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What is Claude's role in legal document review?
To read, extract and summarise document content — the legal judgement stays with the qualified professional. Claude does the reading; you decide what it means and what to do about it.
What should I always tell Claude before asking it to review a contract?
The jurisdiction and the context — for example, "this is a UK commercial supply agreement, we are the buyer." Context shapes what Claude flags as unusual, and produces much sharper output than pasting the document alone.
What must I do before pasting a confidential contract into Claude?
Anonymise it — remove counterparty names, commercially sensitive figures, and any information subject to confidentiality obligations. Use placeholder labels like "Party A" or "Supplier X" instead.
Why must I verify regulatory references Claude includes in legal output?
Because Claude's training data has a cutoff — law and regulations change and may not be current. Any specific article numbers, compliance thresholds or statutory deadlines it references must be verified against current official sources.
Can Claude replace a lawyer for reviewing a contract before signing?
No. Claude does not provide legal advice — it summarises, extracts and drafts. Any contract that will be signed or relied upon must be reviewed by a qualified lawyer in the relevant jurisdiction.
