How to Use Claude AI to Audit and Understand Complex Excel Workbooks

Decode inherited spreadsheets, explain opaque formulas, and document workbooks you didn't build—without spending hours guessing.

5 min read Intermediate Excel 365
Section 1

Where Claude fits in

Most Excel workbooks don't get built all at once—they grow over years. Formulas start referencing other formulas, new sheets keep getting added, old named ranges get forgotten, and eventually the original logic becomes pretty hard to follow. When you inherit one of these monsters—or even come back to something you built months ago—figuring it out can eat up hours.

Here's the good news: Claude can't open Excel files directly, but it's fantastic at working with the information you pull out and share with it—things like formula text, sheet structures, lists of named ranges, and data samples. You describe what you're seeing, paste the relevant bits, and Claude helps you map out the logic, spot the risks, and understand what the workbook is really doing.

This makes it incredibly useful right at the beginning of any project involving an inherited, undocumented, or unusually complex workbook—before you change a single cell.

Here's where Claude shines:

  • You paste a formula and it explains it in plain English, step by step
  • You describe the sheet structure and it helps you see likely dependencies and risks
  • You share a list of named ranges and it tells you which ones are active, orphaned, or might be broken
  • You paste error values and it diagnoses the most likely causes
  • You describe the workbook's purpose and it helps you document it properly
Important Claude isn't a replacement for Excel's built-in auditing tools like Trace Precedents, Name Manager, or the Formula Auditor. It works best alongside them: you use Excel to surface the raw details, and Claude to make sense of them.

1

Map the structure first

Before you paste anything into Claude, take about five minutes in Excel to get a quick structural overview. Note how many sheets there are, what each one seems to contain, which sheets reference other sheets, and whether there are any hidden or very hidden sheets (you may need to check the VBA editor for those).

2

Extract the key formulas

Go to the most complex sheet. Hit Ctrl+` (grave accent) to toggle into formula view and spot the formulas that look most interconnected or hardest to understand. Copy the three to five that worry you most—these are your starting point.

3

Open Name Manager

Go to Formulas → Name Manager. Copy the full list of defined names, including their scope and refers-to values. Paste this into Claude and mention which ones you can't trace to active use.

4

Run a first Claude prompt

Paste your structural overview, the key formulas, and the named range list into Claude. Give it a clear brief: what the workbook is supposed to do and what you need to understand. Ask it to explain the formula logic and flag anything that looks fragile, circular, or potentially broken.

5

Work through the audit iteratively

Claude's first response will give you a solid map and some initial flags. Use that to go back into Excel, check specific cells, and then return to Claude with follow-up questions. The audit turns into a helpful back-and-forth conversation rather than a single big query.

6

Document as you go

Ask Claude to create plain-English summaries of what each key formula does—perfect for a documentation sheet. Paste these back into Excel, either in a new sheet called DOCUMENTATION or as comments on the relevant formulas.

Section 3

Prompts & checklist

Copy these prompts directly into Claude. Fill in the bracketed placeholders with your workbook's specifics.

Core audit prompt

Prompt — paste into Claude
I have an Excel workbook that [describe purpose — e.g. "tracks 
monthly revenue by region and feeds a summary dashboard"]. 
It has [X] sheets: [list sheet names]. I need to understand 
how it works before making changes.

Here are the five formulas I find most complex:
[paste formulas]

Here is the full list from Name Manager:
[paste defined names and refers-to values]

Please: (1) explain what each formula is doing in plain 
English, (2) identify any dependencies between them, 
(3) flag anything that looks fragile, potentially broken, 
or that I should verify before editing.
⌘C / Ctrl+C to copy · replace all [brackets] before sending

Follow-up prompt for documentation

Prompt — paste into Claude
Based on your explanation above, write a plain-English 
documentation block for each formula — one paragraph per 
formula, suitable for a non-technical colleague who needs 
to maintain this workbook. Include: what the formula 
calculates, what inputs it depends on, and any known risks.

Pre-edit safety prompt

Prompt — paste into Claude
I want to change [describe the specific change — e.g. "add 
a new row to the data range on sheet INPUT"]. Based on what 
you know about this workbook's structure, what downstream 
effects should I check before and after making this change?

Audit checklist

  • Sheet inventory complete (including hidden sheets)
  • Key formulas identified and pasted into Claude
  • Named ranges reviewed in Name Manager and shared with Claude
  • Claude explanation reviewed and cross-checked in Excel
  • Circular references checked (Formulas → Error Checking → Circular References)
  • External links checked (Data → Queries & Connections → Edit Links)
  • Documentation sheet created or updated in the workbook
  • Pre-edit safety check done before any structural change
