Step-by-step workflow

Draft a handbook policy with Claude — 5 steps
1

Gather the facts of the policy

Write down the entitlements, eligibility rules, and process steps for the policy area, in your own words or bullet points.

2

Share an example of your handbook style

Provide a short excerpt from an existing policy in your handbook, so Claude can match the tone, structure and heading style.

3

Ask Claude to draft the policy

Share the facts and the style example, and ask for a draft policy section that covers the points you provided.

4

Review for accuracy and legal compliance

Have HR and, where appropriate, legal review the draft against your actual entitlements, contracts and applicable employment law.

5

Approve and publish through your normal process

Once reviewed and approved, add the policy to the handbook following your organisation's usual sign-off and version control process.

Not a legal document: A Claude-drafted policy is a starting point for wording, not a legal document. Employment law varies by country and region, and a published handbook policy can have contractual effect — always have new or changed policies reviewed and approved by HR and legal before publishing.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
Does Claude know our company's actual leave entitlements and policies?
No. Claude does not know your company's specific policies, benefits, or legal obligations. You provide the facts — the entitlements, processes and rules — and Claude helps turn them into clear, well-structured policy text.
What should I give Claude to draft a new handbook policy?
Two things: the facts of the policy (entitlements, eligibility rules, process steps, exceptions) and a short excerpt from an existing policy in your handbook so Claude can match the heading style, tone and structure.
Can Claude simplify a dense policy without changing the rules?
Yes, if you instruct it to. Ask Claude to rewrite the policy in plain English while keeping every entitlement, rule and process exactly as written — changing only the wording, structure and headings, not the substance.
Do I still need legal review before publishing a Claude-drafted policy?
Always. A Claude-drafted policy is a starting point for wording, not a legal document. Employment law varies by country and region, and a published handbook policy can have contractual effect — have HR and legal review and approve it before publishing.
How do I keep a new policy consistent with the rest of the handbook?
Paste a short excerpt from an existing policy into the prompt so Claude can match the heading style, tone and structure. Then check the finished draft against the rest of the handbook before publishing.
