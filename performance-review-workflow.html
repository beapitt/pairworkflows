How to Write a Performance Review with Claude

Turn scattered notes and feedback into a fair, structured performance appraisal — without staring at a blank page. This workflow shows you exactly how to use Claude as your drafting partner, from raw input to polished review.

Free 20 min Freelancers & HR consultants

TL;DR

  • Paste your notes and use one structured prompt — Claude drafts the review.
  • Works on the free plan; no special setup needed.
  • You stay in control: Claude drafts, you verify and personalise.
  • Cuts writing time from hours to under 20 minutes.

Before you start

  • Access to Claude.ai — the free plan is sufficient for this workflow.
  • Your raw notes on the employee: 1-on-1 records, project outcomes, peer feedback, self-assessment responses, or any KPI data you have.
  • A clear idea of the review format your organisation or client expects (sections, length, rating scale if any).
  • The employee's job title, main responsibilities, and the review period — so Claude can contextualise everything correctly.

How to do it — step by step

1

Collect your raw notes and evidence

Before opening Claude, gather everything you have on the employee into one place: 1-on-1 notes, project outcomes, peer feedback summaries, self-assessment answers, and any measurable results. The more specific the input, the more specific the review — bullet points are fine, no need to write full sentences yet. If you use an HR workflow system to track this data through the year, now is when it pays off.

2

Define the review format and tone you need

Decide what structure your organisation or client expects — for example: Overall Summary, Key Achievements, Areas for Development, Goals for Next Period. Also decide on tone: formal and evaluative, coaching-focused, or concise and direct. Include all of this in the prompt you'll write in the next step. If you're working with multiple clients or formats, the HR Prompt Builder can help you save and reuse different configurations.

3

Run the prompt and get a first draft

Open a new Claude conversation. Paste the prompt from the section below, replacing each placeholder with your real data. Claude will read through your notes, identify the key themes and evidence, and produce a structured draft in professional language — typically in under 60 seconds. Don't worry about perfection at this stage; the goal is a solid skeleton, not a final document.

4

Refine tone, evidence, and balance

Read the draft with two questions in mind: Is every positive or constructive claim backed by a specific example from your notes? Does the overall balance feel proportionate to the employee's actual performance? You can ask Claude to adjust in the same conversation — for example: "Make the development section more specific and actionable" or "The opening summary feels too generic — rewrite it based on the Q3 product launch." Keep iterating until the review reflects your genuine assessment. For inspiration on how to frame these follow-up requests, see the wider range of AI for HR Teams resources.

5

Final check and personalise before sharing

Replace any remaining generic phrases with the employee's actual name, project names, dates, and figures. Cross-check every factual claim against your original notes — AI can occasionally misread or blend details, and you are the one signing off on this document. Once satisfied, copy the final text into your HR platform, document template, or review email. The whole process — from pasting notes to ready-to-send review — should take around 20 minutes.

The prompt

You are helping me write a professional performance review for one of my employees or team members.

Context:
- Employee name and job title: [e.g. Sarah Chen, Senior Marketing Coordinator]
- Review period: [e.g. January–December 2024]
- Review format: [e.g. Overall Performance Summary / Key Achievements / Areas for Development / Goals for Next Period]
- Desired tone: [e.g. professional and coaching-focused, not punitive]
- Rating scale (if applicable): [e.g. Exceeds / Meets / Below expectations, or leave blank if no rating]

Raw notes and evidence:
[Paste your notes here — bullet points are fine. Include specific examples, project outcomes, feedback received, metrics, and anything relevant to this employee's performance during the review period.]

Using only the evidence in my notes above, write a structured performance review for this employee. Follow the format I specified. For each section, include specific examples drawn from my notes rather than generic statements. Balance positive observations with honest, constructive feedback. Keep the language professional, clear, and direct — avoid jargon and filler phrases like "goes above and beyond."

Do not invent details that are not in my notes. If a section of the format has no supporting evidence in my notes, say so briefly rather than padding with generic content.

Please produce a draft performance review that I can refine and personalise before sharing with the employee.

What the output looks like

Example output

Overall Performance Summary

Sarah delivered a strong year in the Marketing Coordinator role, taking clear ownership of the Q3 product launch campaign and demonstrating consistent reliability across recurring responsibilities. Her performance meets expectations in most areas and exceeds them in project coordination and cross-team communication.

Key Achievements

Led the end-to-end coordination of the Horizon product launch, managing timelines across four departments and delivering all deliverables on schedule. The campaign generated 2,400 qualified leads in its first month — 20% above the target set in January. Additionally, Sarah redesigned the internal content calendar process, reducing missed deadlines across the team by approximately 30% in H2.

Areas for Development

Sarah's written communication is strong in internal documents but less consistent in external-facing copy, where tone can occasionally drift from the brand voice. A structured review step with the brand team before publishing would address this. She has also flagged a desire to build more confidence in data analysis; enrolling in the Q1 analytics workshop would be a practical next step.

How to customise this workflow

For small business owners with no HR background

If you're writing a performance review for the first time, add this line to the prompt: "Assume I have no HR background — use plain language and avoid jargon." You can also ask Claude to suggest a suitable review format based on your company size and the employee's role before you start drafting. The AI for HR Teams hub has additional guides for non-specialists managing HR tasks solo.

For independent HR consultants working with multiple clients

Create a version of the prompt for each client's review format and save them as templates in your own notes tool. When starting a new review, swap in the client-specific format, rating scale, and competency framework before pasting your notes. The HR Prompt Builder is designed exactly for this: it lets you generate and customise prompts for different HR scenarios without starting from scratch each time.

For 360-degree or peer-inclusive reviews

If you have peer feedback alongside your own manager observations, paste both into the notes section and label each source clearly (e.g. "Peer feedback from design team:", "Manager observations:"). Ask Claude to synthesise across sources and flag any significant discrepancies between self-perception and peer feedback. This gives you a more rounded picture to work from before the review conversation.

For remote or distributed teams

Remote reviews often lack the informal observation data you'd naturally gather in an office. Compensate by including Slack messages, async feedback threads, or written project retrospectives in your notes. Ask Claude to specifically acknowledge collaboration patterns and communication quality — areas that are especially important for remote workers and easy to overlook in a text-based review.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this workflow with the free version of Claude?
Yes. The free plan at claude.ai handles this workflow comfortably for a single review. If you're writing several reviews in one session, you may hit the free-plan message limit — in that case, space them out across a few sessions or upgrade to Claude Pro for uninterrupted access.
Is it safe to paste employee information into Claude?
Claude does not use your conversation data to train its models by default. That said, you should follow your organisation's data handling policies before pasting identifiable employee information into any AI tool. A practical approach: use the employee's first name only, or replace names with role labels (e.g. "the marketing coordinator") if your policy requires it. The output quality is unaffected.
What if I don't have detailed notes — can Claude still help?
Claude can only work with the evidence you provide. If your notes are thin, the draft will be generic — and the prompt specifically instructs Claude not to invent details. Use this as a signal: if you find yourself with little to say at review time, it's worth setting up a lightweight note-taking habit throughout the year. The HR Workflows hub has guidance on building simple tracking systems that make future reviews much easier.
How do I make sure the review doesn't sound AI-generated?
The most effective step is to add specific names, dates, and project references from your own notes — these are the details that make a review feel personal and credible. In the same Claude conversation, you can also ask: "Rewrite any generic phrases to be more specific and direct." After two or three rounds of refinement, the output typically reads as a thoughtful manager's assessment rather than a template.
