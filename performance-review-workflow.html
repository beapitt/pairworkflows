How to Write a Performance Review with Claude

TL;DR
  • Paste peer feedback, KPI data, and manager notes into Claude's prompt template
  • Claude drafts a structured review with summary, competency ratings, and development areas
  • Review the output for accuracy — you validate, Claude drafts
  • Adapt tone and submit: most managers finish in under 20 minutes total

This course gives you a Claude prompt that turns raw manager notes, peer feedback verbatims, and goal data into a complete, balanced performance review — structured for any HR system, ready to edit and submit. No more staring at a blank page at review time.

What to Prepare Before You Open Claude

Claude needs context to produce a useful review. Gather these inputs before you start. The actual writing takes minutes — the preparation is where the work lives, and it would take the same time if you were writing from scratch.

Prerequisites checklist
Peer feedback: verbatim or summarised responses from colleagues who worked directly with the employee. Three to five responses are enough; more is better.
Objective data: KPIs, targets vs actuals, output volume, error rates, customer satisfaction scores — whatever metrics apply to the role.
Manager notes: your own direct observations from the review period — one-to-ones, project outcomes, incidents worth noting.
Self-assessment (optional but valuable): if your company collects one, paste the employee's own written response. It improves the balance of the final review.
Your rating scale: the exact labels used in your HR system (e.g. 1–5, or Exceptional / Exceeds / Meets / Below / Unsatisfactory).
Before pasting: remove or anonymise anything you would not want stored in a third-party system — full legal names in sensitive contexts, health or medical details, or any data your company's AI policy restricts from cloud tools. Check your organisation's policy if unsure.

5-Step Workflow: From Notes to Submitted Review

1

Gather and organise your inputs

Pull together all five input types listed above. If peer feedback arrived in different formats (survey export, email, Slack messages), copy the relevant text into a single document. You do not need to rewrite anything — paste raw notes and Claude will structure them.

2

Fill in the prompt template

Open the HR Prompt Builder or copy the prompt from Section 3 of this course. Replace all bracketed fields: [Employee name], [Role], [Review period], [Rating scale], and paste your inputs into the designated areas. The more specific your inputs, the more specific the review.

3

Run the prompt and read the draft carefully

Send the prompt to Claude and read every sentence of the output. Verify that each claim traces back to something you provided. If Claude has generalised or softened a specific concern, ask it to revise: "The third peer mentioned difficulty meeting deadlines — include that specifically under development areas." Claude iterates on request.

4

Customise tone and add manager voice

Adjust language to match your company's template requirements. Add any context Claude could not know: team dynamics, an informal promotion conversation mid-period, or a circumstance that affected performance (restructure, extended absence). This is also where you confirm the recommended rating before it goes on record. The AI for HR mini-guide has a list of tone-adjustment prompts you can use to make the language more formal, more direct, or more development-focused.

5

Submit and archive inputs

Copy the finalised review into your HR system. Archive your source inputs — the raw peer feedback, KPI data, and notes — separately for at least one full review cycle. You will need them if the employee requests clarification, if the rating is challenged in a calibration session, or if the review feeds into a compensation decision.

Freelancers and independent HR consultants managing reviews for multiple clients: keep a separate input file per employee and never mix inputs across clients in the same Claude session. Start a new conversation for each review.

The Claude Prompt for Writing a Performance Review

Copy this prompt into Claude. Fill in all bracketed fields with your actual data. Claude will return a structured review with an overall summary, goal achievement section, competency ratings, development areas, and a recommended overall rating.

Prompt — paste into Claude
You are helping a manager write a formal performance review. Use only the information I provide below — do NOT invent achievements, weaknesses, or examples I have not mentioned. EMPLOYEE DETAILS: Name: [Employee name] Role: [Job title] Department: [Department] Review period: [e.g. January–December 2025] Manager: [Your name] Rating scale used by our company: [e.g. 1–5, or Exceptional / Exceeds Expectations / Meets Expectations / Below Expectations / Unsatisfactory] GOAL ACHIEVEMENT: [Paste targets vs actuals for each goal set at the start of the period. Include numbers where available.] PEER FEEDBACK (verbatim or summarised): [Paste peer feedback here. Include both positive observations and any concerns raised.] MANAGER OBSERVATIONS: [Paste your own notes from one-to-ones, project outcomes, and direct observations across the period.] SELF-ASSESSMENT (optional): [Paste the employee's own written response if available, or leave blank.] COMPETENCIES TO ASSESS: [List the competencies your company uses, e.g.: Strategic thinking, Collaboration, Communication, Execution & delivery, Leadership (if applicable)] OUTPUT FORMAT: Write the review in the following sections: 1. Overall Summary (2–3 paragraphs) 2. Goal Achievement (bullet format, target vs actual for each goal) 3. Competency Assessment (one paragraph per competency with a rating from the scale above) 4. Development Areas (2–3 specific, actionable items with suggested next steps) 5. Recommended Overall Rating (with one sentence of justification) Tone: professional, balanced, specific. Do not use filler phrases like "a true team player" or "goes above and beyond" unless supported by a specific example from the inputs. Flag with [NEEDS INPUT] any section where you do not have enough information to write confidently.

How to Customise This Workflow

The core prompt works across roles and industries. Adjust these variables to fit your context.

By sector

  • Technology / SaaS: Add a "Technical skills" competency block and paste in code review comments, sprint velocity data, or incident retrospective notes as inputs.
  • Professional services (consulting, legal, accounting): Replace "Goal achievement" with "Client matter outcomes" and include billable hour attainment and client satisfaction scores.
  • Retail / frontline: Add attendance reliability, customer satisfaction scores, and upsell conversion data. Simplify the competency list to five or fewer items.
  • Startups and small businesses: Remove the formal competency section and replace with "What went well / What to improve / What to focus on next period" — lower process overhead, same quality output.
  • Freelancers reviewing contractors: Use the prompt with the [Employee name] field replaced by the contractor's name and [Role] replaced by the project or engagement title. Remove the "Self-assessment" section unless you collected one.

By review type

  • Mid-year check-in: Shorten the goal achievement section to "Progress to date vs target" and omit the recommended overall rating — use "Trajectory" instead (On track / At risk / Off track).
  • Probation review: Add a "Meets the bar for confirmation / Does not yet meet the bar" binary at the end and ask Claude to keep the tone neutral and factual throughout.
  • 360 review: Pass in feedback from upward, peer, and downward sources in clearly labelled sections. Ask Claude to summarise themes across all three directions rather than treating each source separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it ethical to use AI to write a performance review?
Yes, provided you supply accurate inputs and review the output before submitting. Claude drafts — you validate. The manager remains responsible for the final assessment. Using AI to structure and articulate observations is no different from using a template; the judgement is still human.
What inputs does Claude need to write a performance review?
Claude needs: the employee name and role, the review period, your rating scale, peer feedback verbatims, quantitative data (KPIs, targets vs actuals), and your manager observations. The self-assessment is optional but improves balance.
Can Claude handle multiple rating scales (1–5, Exceeds/Meets/Below)?
Yes. Specify your exact scale in the prompt and Claude will frame the summary and competency sections around it. If your company uses a custom label set, paste the label definitions into the prompt context.
How long does this workflow take compared to writing from scratch?
Gathering inputs takes the same time either way — that is the real work. With Claude, the drafting step drops from 30–60 minutes to under 5 minutes. Most managers spend a further 10 minutes reviewing and editing, versus 45–90 minutes writing an equivalent draft unaided.
