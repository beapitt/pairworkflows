Claude AI Prompts That Actually Work

Most people type "summarise this" and wonder why Claude gives them something generic. The problem isn't Claude — it's the prompt. The 4-Pillar Framework and the Refine Loop fix that in under 30 seconds.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
Act as [ROLE — e.g. a senior financial analyst].

Context: [Describe the situation — e.g. our Q3 budget came in 18% under target].

Task: [Describe exactly what you need — e.g. write a 3-bullet executive summary].

Format: [Describe how to present it — e.g. plain English, no jargon, under 100 words].

Fill in all four brackets before you send. Paste into Claude.

Review your last response against my original request. Check: did you follow the role, context, task, and format I specified? If any element is missing or weak, rewrite the response now with those gaps fixed. Do not explain what you changed — just give me the improved version.

Use this the moment an output is 80% right but missing something specific — faster than rewriting from scratch.

Act as a CFO preparing a board update. Summarise our Q3 results in 3 bullets: performance vs target, key risks, and recommended action.

[Paste your Q3 numbers here]

Notice all four pillars are present even in a short prompt: role (CFO), context (board update), task (summarise), format (3 bullets).

Step-by-step workflow

Apply the 4-Pillar Framework — 5 steps
1

Assign the role

Tell Claude who to be: Act as a senior financial analyst. This one line changes vocabulary, priorities, and depth across the entire response.

2

Give the context

State the situation in one sentence: Our Q3 budget came in 18% under target. Without this, Claude is answering a hypothetical instead of your real problem.

3

Define the task precisely

Say exactly what you need: Write a 3-bullet executive summary, not just "summarise this." Precision here removes an entire round of back-and-forth.

4

Specify the format

State the length, tone, and structure: Plain English, no jargon, under 100 words. This is the pillar people skip most often — and the one that saves the most editing time.

5

Run the Refine Loop if it's close but not quite right

Instead of rewriting your prompt, ask Claude to check its own output against your original request and fix only the weak parts. One Refine Loop prompt is almost always faster than starting over.

The 15-word rule: if your prompt has fewer than 15 words, you are almost certainly missing at least two of the four pillars. Add them before you hit send.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What are the four pillars of an effective Claude prompt?
Role, Context, Task, and Format. Role tells Claude who to be, Context tells Claude the situation, Task tells Claude exactly what to do, and Format tells Claude how to present the output. A prompt missing two or more of these usually reads as generic.
Why does a short prompt like "summarise this" produce a generic answer?
Without context, role, or format instructions, Claude has no choice but to guess what you need. If your prompt has fewer than 15 words, you are almost certainly missing at least two of the four pillars — add them before you hit send.
When should I use the Refine Loop prompt?
Use it any time Claude's output is close but missing something specific — roughly 80% right. The Refine Loop asks Claude to check its own response against your original request and rewrite only the weak parts, which is almost always faster than starting the prompt over.
Does the 4-Pillar Framework change depending on my role?
The four pillars stay the same, but the language inside them changes. A Finance prompt and an HR prompt both need Role, Context, Task, and Format — they just use different vocabulary and priorities within each pillar.
Why should I save my best Claude prompts somewhere?
A well-structured prompt is a reusable asset. Once you have a prompt that reliably produces the output you want for a recurring task, saving it in a shared document turns a one-off win into a repeatable process for your whole team.
