Step-by-step workflow

Review a redlined contract with Claude — 5 steps
1

Save the redline as clean text

Grab the redlined version the prospect sent and save it as a plain text file or PDF you can copy from cleanly.

2

Anonymize before pasting anything

Strip out the client's name and specific deal financials. Keep the clause language — that's what Claude actually needs.

3

Name the clauses you care about

Tell Claude to focus only on liability, termination, or payment terms. Constraint keeps the output relevant instead of wandering into formatting notes.

4

Ask for the practical business risk in plain English

Not legal jargon repeated back — the actual impact on your company if the redlined version is accepted.

5

Send the summary to legal, not the decision

Copy the risk summary into an email for your legal team so they see the problem immediately, but let a qualified attorney make the final call.

Never a substitute for legal counsel: AI summaries are just a way to prepare notes for your lawyer. Never sign a contract or make a binding commercial decision based solely on an AI summary without having a qualified attorney review the final language.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
Why is Claude good at reviewing redlined contracts?
Claude follows strict constraints — tell it to only look at Limitation of Liability or Payment Terms and it ignores everything else, sticking to exactly what you asked. It doesn't hallucinate new terms or get creative with legal language, so it flags exactly what changed and explains the practical risk.
Can Claude tell me if a contract change is acceptable for my company?
No. Claude can't interpret your company's specific risk appetite, so it won't tell you whether a change is acceptable for your current business goals. It gives you the comparison and the practical risk in plain English — the acceptability call is yours.
What should I do before pasting a contract into Claude?
Anonymize it. Strip out the client's name and specific deal financials before pasting, and keep the clause language and numbers you actually need reviewed.
Is an AI contract summary a substitute for legal counsel?
No. AI summaries are never a substitute for actual legal counsel — they are a way to prepare notes for your lawyer. Never sign a contract or make a binding commercial decision based solely on an AI summary without a qualified attorney reviewing the final language.
How do I stop Claude from commenting on irrelevant parts of a redline?
Add an explicit constraint to the prompt, such as "Only discuss changes to liability or termination. Ignore formatting or minor phrasing edits." Claude follows scope constraints reliably, so naming the clauses you care about keeps the output focused.
