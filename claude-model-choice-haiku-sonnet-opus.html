The Problem

You open Claude mid-afternoon for something urgent, and there it is: "You've reached your usage limit." The task that actually matters now has to wait.

Almost nobody does this on purpose. It happens because Opus gets used for jobs Haiku would have finished in five seconds, or because every routine email and quick summary gets routed through Sonnet by default — the same model that should be saved for real deliverables.

The daily limit trap: each model draws from the same message budget. Spend it on tasks that don't need heavy reasoning, and there's nothing left when a genuinely complex problem shows up.

The fix isn't using Claude less. It's matching the model to the job, every single time, so your budget lasts until the work that actually needs it.

The fix: a 10-second check before you hit send. Pick the right model first, and stop losing capacity to routine tasks.

Choose the Right Model

Claude Pro and Team plans give you three models built for different jobs. Matching the task to the model is what actually protects your usage limit — not using Claude less.

Haiku
Fast, cheap on your quota, and accurate for anything that's mostly pattern-matching rather than judgment calls.
  • Summarizing meeting notes
  • Classifying customer feedback
  • Translating documents
  • Extracting data from PDFs
  • Cleaning up spreadsheet formatting
Smart Default
Sonnet
The workhorse. Strong reasoning at a reasonable cost — this is where most real business output should happen.
  • Writing reports & emails
  • Analyzing spreadsheet data
  • Writing & debugging code
  • Reviewing contracts and documents
Opus
The most capable, and the most expensive against your limit. Save it for problems that genuinely need deep reasoning.
  • Strategic planning across teams
  • Complex legal or contract analysis
  • High-stakes, multi-step decisions
  • Second attempt when Sonnet falls short
How to decide: default to Haiku for routine extraction and formatting. Move to Sonnet for anything that needs real reasoning or writing. Reach for Opus only when Sonnet's first pass genuinely misses the mark on a hard problem.

How You Burn Your Token Budget

Every message you send draws on your limit through three separate levers — knowing which one you're pulling helps you spend less without doing less.

Input
What you type or paste into the message. A long brief or a pasted email costs more than a short question.
Context
Attached files plus everything said earlier in the chat. Claude re-reads the whole conversation on every reply, so long threads add up fast.
Output
What Claude generates back. A full report costs far more than a three-line summary — ask for only the length you need.
The 10-second rule: before you press send, ask "does this actually need Opus-level reasoning, or will Sonnet — or even Haiku — get it done?" Pick the model. Send. Move on.
