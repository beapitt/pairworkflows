Section 01

Static PDFs slow down client onboarding. NotebookLM turns your product documentation into interactive training assets.

Successfully onboarding a new enterprise client requires them to quickly understand and adopt your software or service. Traditionally, account managers send massive PDF user manuals, slide decks and help center links. Clients rarely read these dense materials, leading to delayed implementations, low product adoption and a high volume of basic support tickets for the account team.

NotebookLM acts as a personalized, AI-driven learning hub to solve this adoption bottleneck. By uploading your official product documentation, implementation guides and technical specs, you create a closed-loop knowledge base. You can then use NotebookLM to instantly generate study guides, frequently asked questions and even conversational audio overviews, transforming static text into engaging, interactive training assets for your buyers.

Key insight: NotebookLM grounds its answers and generated materials strictly in the source documents you manually upload — it will not hallucinate features from the broader internet, ensuring your training materials remain perfectly aligned with your actual product capabilities.

A structured approach to building your Notebook ensures the resulting training materials are accurate, focused and immediately useful to the new client.

1
Curate the source documents
Gather your most up-to-date user manuals, onboarding checklists and technical specifications, saving them as PDFs or Google Docs.
2
Create the dedicated notebook
Import these specific documents into a new NotebookLM project dedicated to either the specific client or the general onboarding phase.
3
Generate training assets
Use the Notebook guide features to automatically generate a study guide, a briefing document and a frequently asked questions list.
4
Create an Audio Overview
Generate an audio discussion based on the sources to provide an alternative, engaging learning format for executive stakeholders.
5
Export and distribute
Copy the generated text assets and download the audio file to share with the client via your standard onboarding portal or email sequence.

Note: While NotebookLM generates highly engaging, conversational Audio Overviews, you cannot currently edit the script, direct the pacing or change the specific voices of the AI hosts.

Using precise prompts within your Notebook ensures the AI extracts the exact setup instructions your client needs, rather than summarizing irrelevant technical background.

Prompt 1 — Role-specific onboarding guide
Act as an implementation specialist. Based exclusively on the uploaded documents, write a step-by-step onboarding guide specifically for a System Administrator. Focus only on the initial configuration, user permission settings, and security setup. Crucial instruction: Include inline citations for every step so I can verify the instructions against the source manual.
Prompt 2 — Extracting common pitfalls
Review the uploaded implementation guides and troubleshooting manuals. Extract the top five most common setup mistakes new users make. For each mistake, provide a one-paragraph solution. Crucial instruction: Base the mistakes and solutions strictly on the text provided. Do not invent troubleshooting steps.
Before distributing NotebookLM-generated training materials
Citation verification: did you click the inline citations to ensure the AI accurately interpreted the original source document?
Audience alignment: is the generated study guide appropriately tailored to the end-user's technical skill level?
Audio accuracy: did you listen to the entire Audio Overview to ensure the AI hosts did not mispronounce critical proprietary product names?
Formatting clarity: have you transferred the generated text into your company's branded templates to maintain a professional appearance?
Completeness check: did the generated FAQ cover all the mandatory setup steps required for a successful implementation?
Important: Never upload unredacted legal contracts, proprietary financial data or sensitive Personally Identifiable Information (PII) into a shared Notebook if you plan to invite external client users, as anyone with access to the Notebook can query and view the underlying source materials.
