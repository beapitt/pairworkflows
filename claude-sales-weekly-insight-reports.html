How to Write a Weekly Sales Report with Claude AI

Paste your CRM export and raw meeting notes into Claude. It synthesizes them into an executive-ready narrative with four structured sections — Executive Summary, Deal Momentum, Emerging Risks, and Leadership Support requests — in under 2 minutes.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
Act as a senior enterprise sales director. Review my raw weekly
notes and CRM data:

[Paste your CRM export and meeting notes here. Tag entries where
 useful — e.g. "Blocker:", "Win:", "Competitor mention:" —
 to help Claude categorize themes accurately]

Synthesize this into a cohesive executive narrative for the VP of Sales.
Use these exact headings:

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
One paragraph on overall territory health and week-on-week momentum.

MAJOR DEAL MOMENTUM
Specific deal-level updates with context on what moved and why.
Not a list of activities — explain the business reason behind each movement.

EMERGING RISKS
Blockers, stalled deals, and early warning signals across the pipeline.
Include the deal name, the specific risk, and the likely impact if unresolved.

REQUIRED LEADERSHIP SUPPORT
Clear, named asks directed at the correct internal stakeholders.
Format: [Ask] → [Directed at] → [Deadline if applicable]

Rules:
- Do not list activities. Weave notes into a flowing narrative.
- Explain the "why" behind deal movements, not just the "what".
- Maintain a professional, objective tone.
- Do NOT invent deal details or pipeline values not in my notes.

Tag your notes with Blocker / Win / Competitor before pasting — dramatically improves Claude's categorization. Works in Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini.

Analyze the unstructured call notes and meeting summaries from
this week:

[Paste your raw notes, call transcripts, or email summaries]

Identify the top three recurring objections or market trends
raised by prospects this week.

For each trend provide:
1. The trend or objection in one sentence
2. Which accounts raised it (use initials or deal codes if needed)
3. A brief narrative explaining the business context — why this
   pattern is emerging now, and what it signals for the pipeline
4. A suggested response or talking point to address it

Rules:
- Do not invent objections not explicitly mentioned in the notes.
- If fewer than three clear trends exist, report what you find
  rather than padding to three.
- Flag if the same objection comes from a single account versus
  multiple — single-account issues are not trends.

Run this alongside the executive narrative — trend patterns inform next week's outreach strategy.

Review my pipeline notes from this week and identify deals
at risk of slipping or going dark.

PIPELINE DATA:
[Paste deal names, stages, last activity dates, and any notes
 on engagement level — CRM export works well here]

For each at-risk deal identify:
1. The specific risk signal (no activity X days / champion went
   quiet / competitor shortlisted / budget freeze mentioned)
2. Probability of slipping to next quarter if unaddressed
3. The single most important next action to re-engage

Output as a table: Deal | Risk Signal | Slip Probability | Next Action

Rules:
- Base risk assessment only on the data I've provided.
- Do NOT assume a deal is healthy just because it's in a late stage.
- Flag if a deal has had zero activity in the last 14 days
  regardless of stage.

Run this on Thursday to give yourself Friday to act on at-risk deals before the week ends.

Step-by-step workflow

Write a weekly sales report with Claude — 5 steps
1

Monday: set strategic goals for the week

At the start of each week, note the 2–3 deals or outcomes that matter most for this reporting cycle. This gives you a mental filter for your note-taking — you capture context that's relevant to the narrative, not every activity.

2

Tue–Thu: log notes with context tags

Keep a running document throughout the week. Tag entries as you go: Blocker:, Win:, Competitor mention:, Champion at risk:. These tags dramatically improve Claude's ability to categorize themes when you paste everything in on Friday.

3

Friday AM: export CRM and combine with notes

Export your weekly activity data from your CRM — calls, emails, stage changes, next steps. Combine it with your running notes document. Scrub any PII or sensitive financial data your company policy restricts from external AI tools before pasting.

4

Friday PM: paste into Claude and generate

Use the Executive narrative prompt above. The synthesis takes under 2 minutes. Claude produces a structured narrative with all four sections — not a bullet list of activities, but a flowing brief that explains what moved and why.

5

Review, personalize, submit

Before sending, verify: do the pipeline numbers match your CRM dashboard? Did Claude correctly capture the nuance on your most sensitive deals? Add your personal perspective on anything Claude couldn't infer from the raw data — internal politics, relationship context, gut read on a prospect's commitment level.

Data privacy: Scrub or anonymize PII, deal financials under NDA, and any data restricted by your company policy before pasting into any external AI tool. Use deal codes instead of client names if needed — Claude works just as well with "Deal A".

Frequently asked questions

What should a weekly sales report include?
Four sections: an Executive Summary of overall territory health, Major Deal Momentum with context on what moved and why, Emerging Risks flagging blockers and stalled deals, and Required Leadership Support with specific named asks. Avoid raw activity lists — leadership needs strategic insight, not a log of calls made.
How does Claude help write a weekly sales report?
Claude synthesizes your CRM export and raw meeting notes into a structured narrative. You paste the unstructured data with the four-section headings specified. Claude weaves the information into a flowing executive brief that explains the "why" behind deal movements — not just the "what".
Can I paste CRM data directly into Claude?
Yes, but scrub sensitive data first. Remove customer PII, exact financial figures under NDA, and any data restricted by your company policy. Use deal codes instead of real account names if needed — Claude produces equally good output with anonymized data.
How long does it take to generate a weekly sales report with Claude?
The synthesis step takes under 2 minutes. The time investment is in data prep — exporting from your CRM and combining with your notes. If you keep a running tagged document throughout the week, Friday aggregation takes about 10 minutes total.
What is the difference between a sales activity report and a sales insight report?
An activity report lists what happened — calls made, emails sent, demos booked. An insight report explains what it means — which deals are accelerating, what objections are recurring, and what leadership needs to unblock. Claude converts activity data into insight narratives.
