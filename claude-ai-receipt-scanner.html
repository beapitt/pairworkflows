The problem with receipts — and why Claude fixes it fast

Turn Claude into an AI Receipt Scanner & Expense Tracker

Receipts pile up. A coffee here, a software subscription there, a client dinner you half-remember. By the time you sit down to do your expenses — or hand things off to your accountant — you've got a pile of paper, a folder of screenshots, and no idea which ones are actually deductible.

Most receipt-scanning apps solve one piece of the puzzle. They extract the data, but they don't categorize it sensibly, flag the deductibles, or output something you can paste straight into a spreadsheet. You end up doing the thinking anyway.

Why Claude Works Better Than a Dedicated App

Claude doesn't need an app. You describe the receipt — or paste the text, or upload a photo if you're on Claude Pro — and it extracts vendor, date, amount, and category in one shot. You can also hand it a whole batch of receipts and ask it to produce a clean expense table ready to paste into your books.

What makes this genuinely useful is that you can customize the logic. Tell Claude your exact category list — the one that matches your accounting software or your tax return — and it'll stick to it. Ask it to flag anything that looks deductible. Ask it to separate personal from business. None of that requires switching between apps or learning a new tool.

  • Extracts vendor, date, amount, and category from pasted receipt text or uploaded images (Claude Pro)
  • Outputs a clean table you can paste directly into Excel or Google Sheets
  • Flags likely tax-deductible items based on your country and business type
  • Handles batch processing — paste 20 receipts at once and get one clean table back
  • Works with your own category list, not a fixed set of app-defined buckets

Ready-to-Use Claude Prompts for Receipt Scanning

Each prompt below handles a different scenario. Copy the one that fits your situation, fill in the placeholders, and paste into Claude. The placeholders are in [square brackets] — replace them with your own details before sending.

Prompt 1 — Single receipt, full extraction

Use this when you have one receipt and want Claude to extract everything neatly and flag whether it's deductible.

Prompt — paste into Claude
I'm a [freelance designer / solopreneur / small business owner] based in [country]. I need to log this receipt for my business expenses. Receipt details: [Paste the receipt text here, or describe it: vendor name, date, items purchased, total amount, payment method] Please extract and return the following in a single table row, formatted so I can paste it into a spreadsheet: - Date (DD/MM/YYYY) - Vendor - Amount ([currency]) - Category (use one of: Software & Subscriptions, Travel, Client Entertainment, Professional Development, Office & Equipment, Marketing, Meals, Other) - Deductible? (Yes / No / Check with accountant) - Notes (any detail worth keeping, e.g. "client name", "50% deductibility rule applies") Do not guess amounts. If any detail is unclear, say so in the Notes column rather than making it up.

Prompt 2 — Batch of receipts, one clean table

Use this at the end of the week or month when you've got a stack of receipts to process at once. Paste them all in, separated by dashes.

Prompt — paste into Claude
I'm a [freelance / solopreneur / small business owner] in [country]. Below are [number] receipts from [month]. I need to categorize them for my business expense records. Use these categories only: Software & Subscriptions, Travel, Client Entertainment, Professional Development, Office & Equipment, Marketing, Meals, Other. For each receipt, produce one row in a table with these columns: Date | Vendor | Amount ([currency]) | Category | Deductible? | Notes Flag anything where deductibility is uncertain as "Check with accountant". Do not invent or assume amounts. If a receipt is incomplete, note what's missing in the Notes column. RECEIPTS: --- [Receipt 1: paste text or describe — vendor, date, items, total] --- [Receipt 2: paste text or describe] --- [Receipt 3: paste text or describe] --- [Add more as needed] After the table, add a one-line summary: total receipts processed, total amount, and total amount flagged as deductible.

Prompt 3 — Monthly expense log with deductible summary

Use this at month-end when you want a formatted expense log and a summary you can hand to your accountant.

Prompt — paste into Claude
I need to create a monthly expense log for [month, year]. I'm a [freelance / solopreneur] in [country or state], and I want to track deductible business expenses. Here are all my expenses for the month: [Paste each expense on a new line: Date, Vendor, Amount, brief description] Please: 1. Build a categorized expense table with columns: Date | Vendor | Amount | Category | Deductible? 2. Group expenses by category and show a subtotal for each 3. At the bottom, add a summary box with: - Total expenses - Total likely deductible - Total to check with accountant - Total personal / non-deductible Use these categories: Software & Subscriptions, Travel, Client Entertainment, Professional Development, Office & Equipment, Marketing, Meals, Other. Format the output so I can paste it directly into Excel.
Tip: On Claude Pro, you can upload a photo of a receipt directly. Just attach the image and use one of the prompts above. Claude will read the text from the photo and produce the same structured output.

4-Step Guide to Adapting These Prompts

The prompts above work out of the box, but your situation will have specifics that make them even more accurate. Here's how to tune them.

01

Replace the category list with your own

The default categories in these prompts are generic. Replace them with the exact categories your accounting software uses — QuickBooks, FreeAgent, Wave, or whatever you're on. Claude will stick to the list you give it. If you use codes (e.g. 6200 – Travel), include the codes. Your accountant will thank you.

02

Specify your country's deductibility rules

Deductibility varies by location. A US freelancer has different rules from a UK sole trader. Add a line to your prompt: I'm a sole trader in the UK — apply HMRC expense rules when flagging deductible items. Claude isn't a tax advisor, but being specific gets you more relevant flags. Always confirm edge cases with your accountant.

03

Add a client or project column if you bill back expenses

If some expenses are billable to clients, add a column to the prompt: Also include a "Client / Project" column. Mark billable expenses with the client name; leave it blank for non-billable. Then list which expenses are associated with which client when you paste them in. This makes invoice reconciliation much faster.

04

Build a running log by pasting output into one spreadsheet

Claude doesn't store anything between sessions. Your running expense log lives in a spreadsheet. Each time you process a batch, paste Claude's output table into the next empty row of your sheet. By month 3, you'll have a full-year record building itself one batch at a time — no app subscription needed.

Important: Claude is not a tax advisor. Use its deductibility flags as a starting point, not a final answer. Anything flagged "Check with accountant" should go to your accountant before you file.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Claude read photos of receipts?
If you're using Claude.ai Pro or the web app, you can upload a photo of a receipt directly. Claude will extract the vendor, date, amount, and category. On the free plan, type or paste the receipt details as text instead.
How accurate is Claude at reading handwritten receipts?
Claude handles printed receipts well. Handwritten receipts are hit-or-miss depending on legibility. For handwritten receipts, it's faster to type the key details — vendor, date, and amount — and let Claude handle the categorization.
What expense categories should I use?
Use the categories that match your tax return or accounting software. Common freelancer categories include Software & Subscriptions, Travel, Client Entertainment, Professional Development, Office & Equipment, and Marketing. Tell Claude your category list in the prompt and it will stick to it.
Can I use this to track expenses across the whole year?
Yes — run the prompt in batches. Process a month at a time, paste the output into a running spreadsheet, and you'll have a full-year log by December. Claude doesn't store anything between sessions, so the spreadsheet is your record.
What's the best way to handle receipts in foreign currencies?
Include the exchange rate in your prompt, or ask Claude to flag foreign-currency items separately so you can apply the correct rate yourself. Specify your home currency and the date of the transaction so the conversion is accurate.
