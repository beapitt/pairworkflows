Step-by-step workflow

Catch churn risk with Claude — 5 steps
1

Collect recent communications

Grab the last month of emails and open support desk conversations from the account. You need enough volume to spot a pattern, not just one grumpy message.

2

Define the baseline

Figure out how the client usually talks — were they enthusiastic during onboarding, or always strictly business? Without this, you can't tell a real shift from normal tone.

3

Paste both periods into Claude

Drop the older baseline messages and the recent threads into the prompt window together, so Claude can compare them directly instead of judging tone in isolation.

4

Identify the shifts

Ask Claude to point out any changes in patience, tone, or willingness to collaborate — and to quote the exact sentences instead of giving you a vague impression.

5

Flag for action, not for panic

Turn the findings into a specific next step — a proactive check-in call, not a generic "just checking in" email. The goal is to close the gap while it's still small.

Validate before you escalate: Sentiment analysis is a directional signal, not a definitive diagnosis. Always confirm the read with a direct conversation with the client before escalating the account internally as a churn risk.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What is Claude's main advantage for detecting churn risk?
Claude spots semantic nuance and passive-aggressive tone shifts that keyword-based trackers miss. It understands the context and emotional weight behind phrasing rather than just scanning for angry keywords, so it catches quiet disengagement weeks before a client explicitly complains.
What is a direct limitation of Claude when analyzing client sentiment?
Claude can't know if a champion just got a new boss, is dealing with an internal budget cut, or is simply having a bad personal week. It reads the text you give it, not the real-world context behind it, so you have to factor that in yourself before acting.
What should I do right after Claude flags a negative tone shift?
Validate the finding with a direct conversation with the client before escalating internally. Sentiment analysis is a directional signal, not a definitive diagnosis, so read the flagged messages yourself and confirm you agree with the AI's reading before raising the alarm.
Why does defining a sentiment baseline matter before running this workflow?
You need to know how a client normally communicates to recognize when their tone actually drops. A client who is always terse isn't shifting; a client who was warm and chatty during onboarding and has gone flat and one-word is. The baseline is what makes the shift visible.
How often should I run a churn-risk sentiment scan on a key account?
Monthly for standard accounts, and after any major event — a renewal approaching, a support escalation, a champion change — for strategic accounts. Running it reactively only after a client goes quiet defeats the purpose of catching risk early.
