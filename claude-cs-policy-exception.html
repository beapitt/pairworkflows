Step-by-step workflow

Evaluate a policy exception with Claude — 5 steps
1

Receive the exception request

Grab the client's email or support ticket detailing exactly what non-standard thing they're asking for. Don't paraphrase it — keep the original wording.

2

Locate the relevant policy

Dig out your official internal terms, SLA, or standard operating procedure that governs this specific area. Confirm it's the current version, not an old draft.

3

Paste both into Claude

Drop the client's request and the exact policy text into the prompt window. The comparison is only as accurate as the source text you provide.

4

Generate the evaluation

Ask Claude to identify exactly where the request clashes with your rules, and to flag any policy gap separately from a clear conflict.

5

Escalate with a recommendation

Take Claude's breakdown, add your own read on the account's strategic value, and send a clean, objective recommendation to your manager.

Human authorization required: Claude provides structured analysis to help you think through a problem, but it does not have the authority to grant commercial, legal, or financial exceptions. Final decisions always go through a person.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What is Claude's primary role when dealing with an edge-case request?
Claude acts as an objective sounding board. You give it the exact text of your internal policy and the customer's request, and it weighs the request against the rules without being influenced by the client's emotional tone.
What is a clear limitation of Claude when evaluating policy exceptions?
Claude can't know your company's unwritten historical precedents, like the fact that you always bend a rule for Fortune 500 accounts. You have to explicitly tell it if those unofficial rules apply.
Can Claude approve or deny an exception request?
No. Final exception decisions always require human authorization. Claude provides structured analysis to help you think through the request, but it does not have the authority to grant commercial, legal, or financial exceptions.
What is the first step in evaluating a policy exception with Claude?
Grab the client's exact exception request and locate the relevant internal policy, terms, or SLA that governs it. Paste both into Claude so it has the specific rule text to compare the request against.
How does Claude tell the difference between a clear conflict and a grey area?
Claude compares the request line by line against the policy text. If the request directly contradicts an explicit clause, it flags a clear conflict. If the policy simply doesn't address the situation, it flags a policy gap rather than forcing a false conflict.
