Section 01

Logistics coordination means a lot of written updates. Gemini can draft them from the details you provide.

Coordinating shipments, managing suppliers and communicating delivery updates means writing the same types of message over and over[cite: 12]. Supply chain professionals can spend hours drafting status updates and transit instructions when that time could go to solving actual routing problems and managing active disruptions[cite: 12].

Gemini can take on the drafting step[cite: 12]. Give it the tracking details, ETAs and delay reasons you already have, and it can generate a clear customer update, a structured supplier instruction or an internal team summary in seconds — keeping all stakeholders informed without the administrative bottleneck[cite: 12].

Key insight: Gemini processes only the transit information you provide[cite: 12]. It does not connect to live Transportation Management Systems or track shipments in real time[cite: 12].
Shipment status updates
Generate clear emails informing stakeholders about transit delays or revised delivery schedules[cite: 12].
Supplier coordination
Draft structured messages detailing loading requirements and pickup schedules for vendors[cite: 12].
Internal logistics summaries
Summarise daily dispatch logs and carrier data into readable reports for the operations team[cite: 12].
Always include these details in a logistics prompt
Reference numbers — order IDs, tracking numbers, bill of lading references[cite: 12]
Dates and ETAs — exact pickup windows, estimated arrival dates, delay duration[cite: 12]
Recipient context — whether the message is for a customer, supplier or internal team[cite: 12]
Without Gemini
Copy and paste tracking numbers and ETAs into repetitive emails manually, risking miscommunication and delays when urgent issues arise[cite: 12].
With Gemini
Turn raw transit data into professional, structured updates quickly, freeing up time to handle active supply chain disruptions[cite: 12].

A consistent workflow for AI-assisted logistics communication ensures critical transit details are always accurate and the right people are kept informed[cite: 12].

1
Gather the shipping details
Collect the relevant tracking information, ETAs and delay reasons directly from your carriers[cite: 12].
2
Define the recipient
Identify whether the message is for a supplier, a customer or the internal warehouse team to set the right tone[cite: 12].
3
Draft the update with Gemini
Paste the raw transit data into Gemini and ask it to generate a structured message or status report[cite: 12].
4
Review for accuracy
Verify that all dates, locations and reference numbers in the draft are completely correct[cite: 12].
5
Send the communication
Copy the finalised text into your email client or messaging system and notify the stakeholders[cite: 12].

Note: Gemini does not know standard transit times between specific locations — always provide the exact dates and anticipated delays in your prompt rather than expecting it to calculate them[cite: 12].

Clear parameters in your prompts keep Gemini focused on the information you have provided and prevent it from inventing shipping milestones or ETAs[cite: 12].

Prompt 1 — Customer shipment status update
Act as a logistics coordinator. Draft an email to a customer updating them on their shipment: [Insert Order and Tracking Details]. Explain that there is a delay due to [Insert Delay Reason]. Crucial instruction: Base the email strictly on the provided information. Do not invent new ETAs, offer compensation, or create tracking milestones not included in the text.[cite: 12]
Prompt 2 — Supplier pickup instructions
Draft a structured message to our supplier regarding an upcoming pickup: [Insert Pickup Date, Location, and Reference Number]. Specify the following loading requirements: [Insert Requirements]. Crucial instruction: Only include the details provided. Do not add unverified warehouse rules, external contact numbers, or delivery windows not stated in this prompt.[cite: 12]
Before sending a Gemini-drafted logistics update
Reference accuracy: do all tracking numbers, purchase orders and bill of lading references match the source data?[cite: 12]
Date precision: are the ETAs and pickup windows stated exactly as provided by the carrier?[cite: 12]
Tone: is the language professional and appropriately reassuring when communicating a delay?[cite: 12]
Clarity: are the instructions for the warehouse team or supplier clear and unambiguous?[cite: 12]
Placeholders removed: have all bracketed fields been replaced with the correct shipping details?[cite: 12]
Important: International shipments involve specific legal shipping terms such as incoterms (FOB, EXW and others)[cite: 12]. Always verify that Gemini has not inadvertently altered or misrepresented these terms in the draft — doing so can unintentionally shift liability for the freight[cite: 12].
