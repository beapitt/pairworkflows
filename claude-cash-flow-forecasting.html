How to Use Claude AI for Cash Flow Forecasting Narratives

Claude doesn't calculate your forecast — your spreadsheet does that. What it does is turn the output into a clear scenario narrative, documented assumptions, and commentary that explains what's driving the numbers, ready for people who need to understand it, not just see it.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
Act as a Finance Manager. Write a narrative summary of the following
13-week cash flow forecast for senior management.

Opening cash position: [figure]
Weekly cash in/out summary: [Paste your weekly figures — inflows and
outflows]
Lowest projected cash week: [Week number and figure]
Key assumptions: [e.g. "Customer X payment assumed in week 6", "No new
funding drawn"]

Format: one paragraph overview of the 13-week trend, a flag for the
lowest cash point with [WATCH] if it falls below [your minimum
threshold], and a short list of the key assumptions underlying the
forecast.

Replace all bracketed sections with your real data. Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini.

Act as a Finance Business Partner. Write a scenario narrative comparing
the following three cash flow scenarios.

Base case — closing cash position and key assumptions: [figures and
assumptions]
Best case — closing cash position and what changes from base: [figures
and assumptions]
Worst case — closing cash position and what changes from base: [figures
and assumptions]

Format: one short paragraph per scenario explaining what drives the
difference from the base case, followed by a summary sentence on the
range of outcomes. Plain language — suitable for a non-finance board
audience.

Claude explains what drives each scenario — it doesn't recommend which one to pursue. That call stays with you.

Act as a Financial Controller. Turn the following list of forecast
assumptions into a clear, structured assumptions document for the
forecast model.

Assumptions (in any order): [List each assumption — e.g. "Revenue grows
3% month on month", "Payment terms remain 30 days", "No new hires before
Q3"]
Forecast period: [e.g. 13 weeks / FY2026]
Owner of the forecast: [Role title]

Format: group assumptions under headings (Revenue, Costs, Working
Capital, Other). For each assumption, state it clearly in one sentence
and note whether it is conservative, neutral or optimistic relative to
recent actuals.

Written once, this document gets reused every forecast cycle — and makes it obvious when an assumption has quietly gone stale.

Step-by-step workflow

Build the cash flow narrative workflow with Claude — 5 steps
1

Build and run your model first

Your cash flow model — formulas, links, scenario toggles — stays in Excel or Google Sheets. Claude does not build or verify the model itself.

2

Export the output, not the formulas

Paste the resulting figures — opening position, weekly movements, scenario outcomes — into Claude. The formulas and underlying model stay in your spreadsheet.

3

Always include the assumptions

Every figure should come with the assumption behind it. This is what lets Claude write a narrative that explains the why, not just the what.

4

State your minimum cash threshold

To flag a low point as [WATCH], Claude needs to know what counts as low for your business. State it explicitly rather than letting Claude guess a generic figure.

5

Verify the narrative against the model

Read the output back against your model before sharing. Claude should describe what your forecast shows — if it doesn't match, the input was incomplete or unclear.

Important: Claude does not replace your forecasting model or your judgement on funding decisions. It produces narrative and documentation from figures you provide — every figure and assumption must be verified against your model before the forecast is shared or acted upon.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What is Claude's role in cash flow forecasting?
Claude writes the narrative and documentation around a forecast — it does not calculate the forecast itself. Your cash flow model, formulas, and scenario toggles stay in your spreadsheet. Claude turns the output into assumptions documentation, scenario narratives, and commentary for stakeholders.
What should I always include alongside the figures when prompting Claude?
The assumptions behind each figure — the why, not just the what. A forecast figure without its assumption is just a number. Claude needs to know what's driving the number to write a narrative that actually explains the forecast, not just restates it.
In a best/base/worst case scenario prompt, what does Claude explain for each scenario?
What drives the difference from the base case, in plain language suitable for a non-finance audience. Claude does not choose which scenario the company should pursue — it explains the assumptions and outcomes so stakeholders can make that call themselves.
What must happen before a Claude-generated forecast narrative is shared?
Every figure and assumption must be verified against the underlying model before the forecast is shared or acted upon. Claude produces narrative and documentation from figures you provide — it does not replace your forecasting model or your judgement on funding decisions.
Should I paste my spreadsheet formulas into Claude?
No. Export the output — opening cash position, weekly movements, scenario outcomes — not the formulas or the underlying model. The model and its logic stay in your spreadsheet; Claude only needs the resulting figures and their assumptions to write the narrative.
