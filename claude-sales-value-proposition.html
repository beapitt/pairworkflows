How to Write a Value Proposition with Claude AI

Paste your product features and the buyer persona you're targeting into Claude. It maps each feature to that persona's specific pain points and rewrites your pitch as outcome-focused statements — then stress-tests it by acting as a skeptical prospect.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
Act as an enterprise sales strategist. Here are my raw product
features and differentiators:

FEATURES:
[List your features as bullet points — don't worry about flow,
 just get the facts down]

TARGET BUYER:
Role: [e.g. CFO / VP Operations / IT Director]
Their primary business goal: [e.g. reduce costs / improve reliability]
Their specific pain point: [e.g. too much manual reconciliation
 eating into team capacity]
Industry: [e.g. mid-market SaaS / manufacturing / financial services]

Rewrite my value proposition so it speaks directly to this person.

Rules:
- Avoid generic marketing speak.
- Focus on clear business outcomes, not feature specs.
- Use the professional register of this buyer — a CFO speaks
  differently from an IT Director.
- Keep the core statement to 2 sentences maximum.
- Add 3 supporting proof points (each one sentence).
- Do NOT invent capabilities I haven't listed in the features above.

The more specific your pain point, the less generic the output. Works in Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini.

Review the value proposition below. Act as a cynical prospect

[Paste the pitch Claude just wrote, or your existing value prop]

Give me:
1. The 3 most likely objections this buyer would raise to this pitch
2. Why each objection undermines the current message
3. A rewritten version of the pitch that handles all 3 objections
   proactively — without making it longer or more defensive

Rules:
- Do not be defensive about the original pitch.
- Make the narrative stronger by addressing the concerns directly.
- If a feature claim is vague or unverifiable, flag it — do not
  paper over it with better-sounding language.

Step-by-step workflow

Write a value proposition with Claude — 5 steps
1

Define one specific persona per run

Don't try to write a value proposition for "decision-makers" in general. Pick one person — their role, their primary business goal, and a specific pain point they've actually mentioned. The more specific your input, the less generic the output. Claude can't infer what a CFO cares about from a job title alone.

2

List raw features — don't pre-translate them

Write your features as plain facts: "connects via API in under 4 hours", "SOC 2 Type II certified", "reduces manual reconciliation steps from 12 to 3". Don't try to make them sound good yet — that's Claude's job. Pre-translated features limit what Claude can do with them.

3

Run the persona-specific pitch prompt

Use the first tab above. Claude maps your features to the persona's pain points and rewrites them as outcome-focused statements in that buyer's professional language. A CFO pitch uses different words than an IT Director pitch — Claude handles this automatically when you specify the persona clearly.

4

Stress-test with the objection prompt

Immediately run the objection testing prompt on the output. Ask Claude to act as a cynical version of that buyer and identify the 3 most likely objections. Then have it rewrite the pitch to handle those objections proactively. Do this before your first call — not after a lost deal.

5

Align with marketing before going client-facing

AI-generated messaging must match your company's officially approved marketing and legal language. Use Claude to draft and iterate quickly — then get sign-off from marketing before the message goes into a deck, email, or proposal. Never let AI output go to a client without a human review.

Tip: Claude doesn't know your secret sauce — the unique angle that differentiates you from competitors. Feed that explicitly into the prompt: "Our key differentiator versus [Competitor] is [specific thing]." Without it, Claude produces a pitch that could apply to any vendor in your category.

Frequently asked questions

What is a value proposition in sales?
A value proposition is a clear statement that explains how your product solves a specific buyer's problem, what outcome they get, and why they should choose you over alternatives. It is not a feature list — it is a translation of features into business outcomes that matter to the specific person you're talking to.
How does Claude help write a value proposition?
Claude maps your raw features to a specific buyer persona's priorities and rewrites them as outcome-focused statements. You provide the features and persona context — Claude produces the narrative. It also acts as a skeptic to test whether the resulting pitch handles the most likely objections before you use it on a real call.
Can I use the same value proposition for every buyer?
No. A CFO cares about cost and risk. A VP Operations cares about speed and reliability. An IT Director cares about security and integrations. The same product feature needs different framing for each persona. Use the Multi-persona matrix tab above to generate all three versions in one prompt run.
How do I avoid generic AI-generated marketing language?
Include "Avoid generic marketing speak — focus on clear business outcomes in a direct, professional tone" in your prompt. Also give Claude specific pain points the persona has mentioned in real conversations, not just their job title. The more specific your input, the less generic the output.
Should I run AI-generated messaging past my marketing team?
Always. AI-generated messaging must align with your company's officially approved marketing and legal language before it goes in front of a client. Use Claude to draft and iterate quickly — then get sign-off before using in client-facing decks or proposals.
