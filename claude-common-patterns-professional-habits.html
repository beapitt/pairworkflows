Step-by-step workflow

Break the five habits — 5 steps
1

Start a new conversation for each distinct task

The rule: one conversation per deliverable. If a task has a different output than the last task, it earns a new conversation. Use Claude Projects to hold persistent context — brand voice, instructions — without dragging old conversations along.

2

Paste only the relevant section, not the whole file

You do not need to hand over the whole document to get the chapter summary. Copy the pages or paragraphs that contain what you need. "Focus on X" while uploading the full file does not reduce the token cost.

3

Invest 60 seconds upfront on the prompt

Specify audience, tone, length, format, and what "good" looks like for this output. One focused, well-scoped prompt reliably beats four vague ones — in output quality and efficiency.

4

Ask "does a dedicated tool already do this?"

Before opening Claude for a task, check whether Excel, your CRM, or Figma already does it in under 30 seconds. Reserve Claude for synthesis, drafting, analysis, and reasoning — where thinking is the actual challenge.

5

Diagnose before you regenerate

Identify the specific element that's wrong — tone, structure, opening line, length — and give Claude that feedback explicitly. Directed revision beats random regeneration every time.

Token arithmetic: three regenerations cost three times as much as one targeted revision instruction. The revision also produces a better output, because it responds to a specific diagnosis rather than starting from scratch with random variation.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
Why does staying in one long Claude conversation all week hurt output quality?
Claude re-reads the entire conversation from message one every time you hit send. A conversation that has accumulated context from multiple unrelated projects makes each new response carry and process a heavy, mixed history, which produces responses that feel slower and more generic over time.
Does telling Claude to focus on one section reduce the token cost of a full document upload?
No. Telling Claude to focus on the executive summary while uploading the full document does not reduce the token load — the entire document is still processed. Only pasting the relevant section directly avoids that cost.
What is the most efficient way to fix a vague Claude output?
Rewrite the original prompt with specific audience, tone, length, and format, and give an example of what good looks like. This beats a draft-and-revise loop of asking for "more professional" then "shorter" — one well-scoped prompt reliably beats several vague follow-ups.
When should I use a dedicated tool instead of Claude?
When a dedicated tool already does the task in under 30 seconds — reformatting a spreadsheet, converting a file, resizing an image, building a pivot table. Reserve Claude for synthesis, drafting, analysis, and reasoning, where thinking and language are the actual challenge.
Is it better to regenerate a Claude response or give targeted feedback?
Targeted feedback is almost always better. Diagnose what specifically is wrong — tone, structure, opening line, length — and give Claude that feedback explicitly. Three regenerations cost three times as much as one targeted revision instruction, and the revision produces a better result because it responds to a specific diagnosis.
