ROLE
You are a financial analyst building a loan amortization schedule in Excel.

OBJECTIVE
Generate a complete amortization table showing the payment breakdown for every period until the loan is paid off.

LOAN PARAMETERS:
- Principal: [e.g. £150,000]
- Annual interest rate: [e.g. 5.0% nominal, compounded monthly]
- Loan term: [e.g. 5 years]
- Payment frequency: [e.g. Monthly]
- First payment date: [e.g. 01/08/2025]

CONSTRAINTS
- Calculate the periodic rate as Annual rate ÷ Number of payments per year.
- Use the standard annuity formula: PMT = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1].
- Carry all calculations to at least 6 decimal places internally. Round displayed amounts to 2 decimal places.
- The final period's balance must be exactly 0.00 (adjust the last payment if rounding creates a residual).
- Do NOT invent loan terms.

OUTPUT
1. A tab-separated table with columns: Period | Payment Date | Payment | Principal | Interest | Remaining Balance.
2. A summary showing Total payments, Total interest paid, and Total principal repaid.

Replace the bracketed values with your loan terms. Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini.

I need a loan amortization schedule that accounts for lump-sum prepayments.

LOAN PARAMETERS:
- Principal: [e.g. £150,000]
- Annual interest rate: [e.g. 5.0% nominal, compounded monthly]
- Loan term: [e.g. 5 years]
- Payment frequency: [e.g. Monthly]

EXTRA PAYMENTS:
[e.g. Period 12: £5,000 extra principal; Period 24: £10,000 extra principal]

CONSTRAINTS
- The regular payment (PMT) remains fixed. 
- Extra payments immediately reduce the outstanding principal balance.
- Recalculate the interest component for all subsequent periods based on the new, lower balance.
- Because of the prepayments, the loan will be paid off early. Stop the schedule when the balance reaches zero.
- The final payment should only be whatever is needed to bring the balance to exactly zero.

OUTPUT
- A tab-separated table with columns: Period | Regular Payment | Extra Payment | Total Principal | Interest | Remaining Balance.
- A summary showing: Total interest paid, and Interest saved (compared to no extra payments).

Use this to accurately model the interest savings and shortened term resulting from prepayments.

Step-by-step workflow

Build the schedule with Claude
1

Confirm the rate basis

Is your rate nominal or effective? Most agreements quote a nominal annual rate. If it's "5% per annum compounded monthly," the periodic rate is 5% ÷ 12 = 0.4167%. State this clearly in the prompt.

2

Decide on extra payments upfront

If you plan to model prepayments, list each one with its period number. Claude will apply the extra payment to principal immediately and recalculate interest for every subsequent period, showing the exact interest saved.

3

Handle variable rates explicitly

For loans with rate resets (e.g., fixed for 24 months, then floating), provide a simple table. Claude will recalculate the payment amount at each step change based on the remaining balance, keeping the math sound.

4

Verify the three golden rules

Check the output: (1) First period interest = Principal × periodic rate. (2) Final balance = 0.00. (3) Sum of all principal payments = Original loan amount. If these hold true, the schedule is correct.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
Can Claude handle variable interest rates in an amortization schedule?
Yes. Include a rate schedule in your prompt — for example, 4.5% for months 1–24, then 5.2% from month 25 onward. The prompt instructs Claude to recalculate the payment amount at each rate change so principal and interest splits remain accurate.
How does Claude calculate the monthly payment amount?
Claude uses the standard annuity formula: PMT = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1], where P is the principal, r is the periodic interest rate, and n is the total number of payments. This is the same formula behind Excel's PMT function.
Can I model extra principal payments or lump-sum prepayments?
Yes. Add the period number and amount to the Extra Payments section of the prompt. Claude reduces the outstanding balance immediately and recalculates interest for all subsequent periods, showing you exactly how much interest the prepayment saves.
Will the schedule work for quarterly or annual payment frequencies?
Yes. Specify the frequency in the prompt — monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, or annual. Claude adjusts the periodic rate and number of periods. A 5-year loan at 6% annual with quarterly payments becomes 20 periods at 1.5% per period.
How do I verify Claude's amortization output?
Check three things: the first payment's interest equals the principal multiplied by the periodic rate; the final remaining balance is exactly zero; and the sum of all principal payments equals the original loan amount. If any of these fail, the schedule has an error.
