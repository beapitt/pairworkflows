Step-by-step workflow

Adapt these prompts to your situation — 5 steps
1

Use your real take-home number, not your gross salary

If your income varies (freelance, hourly, commission), use the lowest month from the past six as your base. You can always revise upward in a good month; you can't unspend in a bad one.

2

Add a savings goal with a deadline

Make the budget prompt more concrete: I want to save $5,000 for a house deposit by [month, year]. How much do I need per month, and what does that do to my remaining budget?

3

Remove card and account numbers before pasting a spending review

Claude only needs the date, a description, and the amount — nothing else. Delete any columns with card numbers or account IDs from your bank export first.

4

Ask Claude to prioritize, not split evenly

Add: I can only allocate $[X] per month beyond fixed expenses. Rank emergency fund, debt payoff, and savings — and tell me why. Claude will rank by interest rate and risk.

5

Run the spending review every month

Paste the output into one running spreadsheet — one tab per month, one summary tab. After three months you'll have enough data to see real patterns.

Important: Claude is not a financial advisor. The calculations it produces are based solely on the numbers you provide. For decisions involving tax, investments, or significant debt restructuring, speak to a qualified financial professional.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
Is it safe to share my financial details with Claude?
Claude doesn't store your data between sessions. What you paste into a conversation isn't saved or used to train the model. That said, use common sense — there's no need to include account numbers, card numbers, or any identifying information. Amounts and categories are enough for every budgeting task.
Can Claude connect to my bank account?
No. Claude doesn't have access to your bank. You export or copy your transaction data and paste it in. It's a manual step, but it also means you're in control of exactly what Claude sees.
What's the best way to track spending categories over time?
Run the monthly spending review prompt each month and paste Claude's output into a single running spreadsheet. After three months you'll have enough data to spot real patterns — which categories creep up, which months are expensive, and whether your savings rate is actually moving.
Can Claude help me choose between paying off debt and saving?
Yes. Give Claude your debt interest rates and your savings account rate, and ask it to compare the two scenarios mathematically. In most cases, high-interest debt costs more to carry than a savings account earns. Claude will show you the numbers for your specific situation.
What if my income varies month to month?
Use your lowest recent month as the base for your budget, not your average. Tell Claude your income range in the prompt and ask it to build a conservative budget that works on the low end. You can always spend more in a good month — you can't unspend in a bad one.
