How to Build a Payroll Reconciliation Template in Excel with Claude AI

Paste your payroll export and GL extract into Claude and get back a line-by-line reconciliation — matched items confirmed, variances flagged with probable cause, off-cycle items listed separately, and a sign-off summary ready for month-end close.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
PAYROLL SYSTEM TOTALS (aggregated by Cost Centre and GL Account):
[Paste your payroll data here — columns: Cost Centre | GL Account | Pay Type | Amount]

GENERAL LEDGER POSTINGS (for the same period):
[Paste your GL extract here — columns: Journal Ref | Cost Centre | GL Account | Description | Amount]

GL ACCOUNT CODE MAPPING (only if payroll uses different codes from GL):
[e.g. "6100-WAGES" in payroll = "6100" in GL — leave blank if codes match]

RECONCILIATION SETTINGS:
- Tolerance: £[e.g. 0.50] — differences within this amount = MATCH
- Group by: Cost Centre and GL Account
- Period: [e.g. June 2025]

Rules:
- Match payroll totals to GL postings by Cost Centre AND GL Account.
  Do not match across accounts.
- Variance = GL Posted minus Payroll Total. Negative = GL understated.
- Payroll line with no GL match → MISSING FROM GL
- GL entry with no payroll match → UNMATCHED GL ENTRY
- Equal-and-opposite variances across accounts (e.g. 6100 is -£2,500
  and 6200 is +£2,500) → flag as PROBABLE MISCODING, not two issues.
- List all off-cycle pay items in a separate section at the bottom.
- Do NOT invent figures or infer what the correct amount should be.

Output:
1. Reconciliation table: Cost Centre | GL Account | Pay Type |
   Payroll Total | GL Posted | Variance | Status
2. Equal-and-opposite variance summary (if any)
3. Off-cycle items list
4. Sign-off summary: Total payroll | Total GL | Net variance |
   Number of unreconciled items

Step-by-step workflow

Run a payroll reconciliation with Claude — 4 steps
1

Export payroll totals by cost centre and GL account

From your payroll system, export a summary by cost centre and pay type — not individual employee rows. You need: Cost Centre, GL Account, Pay Type (Gross Pay / Employer NIC / Pension), and Amount. Most payroll systems have a cost analysis or cost centre summary report for this.

2

Export the matching GL postings for the same period

From your accounting system, extract all journal lines posted to payroll GL accounts (typically 6100–6399 or equivalent) for the period. Include Journal Ref, Cost Centre, GL Account, Description, and Amount. If your payroll and GL use different account codes, prepare a mapping table.

3

Paste both datasets into the prompt and set your tolerance

A tolerance of £0.50 handles rounding differences between systems without flagging them as real variances. Set it to £0.00 if you need exact matching. Specify the period clearly — Claude uses it in the sign-off summary header.

4

Check equal-and-opposite variances first

When one account is short by exactly the amount another is over, the total payroll cost is correct but the expense split by type is wrong. This is a GL miscoding — one correcting journal fixes both variances simultaneously. Claude flags these automatically with PROBABLE MISCODING so you don't investigate them as two separate issues.

Tip: Run payroll reconciliation before bank reconciliation, not after. Confirming that GL postings are correct first makes the bank rec faster — you're only checking that net pay cleared the account, not hunting for coding errors at the same time.

Step-by-step workflow

Investigate and fix a variance — 4 steps
1

Identify the specific Cost Centre and GL Account

Use the main reconciliation output to find the exact combination causing the variance (e.g., Operations - 6100 Gross Pay).

2

Pull the GL transaction list

Export only the ledger entries for that specific GL account and cost centre for the period. Do not clutter the prompt with the entire company's ledger.

3

Provide Claude with the details

Use the Variance prompt and supply the payroll total, GL total, and the detailed GL rows. Claude checks against common issues like missing journals, wrong period postings, or manual adjustments.

4

Post the correcting journal

Depending on Claude's diagnosis, post the appropriate reclass, accrual, or missing entry in your ERP system to bring the GL in line with the actual payroll run.

Step-by-step workflow

Reconcile at the employee level — 4 steps
1

Export raw payroll data per employee

Export a detailed payroll register showing Gross Pay, Deductions, and Net Pay tagged with the unique Employee ID.

2

Ensure GL report includes Employee ID tags

Your GL entries must have the Employee ID mapped in a subledger or reference field. Without this, line-by-line employee matching is impossible.

3

Run the strict Employee ID matching prompt

Paste both datasets into Claude using the Employee-level prompt. Claude strictly links the records by ID and Account to identify missing or mismatched figures.

4

Follow up on orphaned records

Investigate the exceptions list. Orphaned records often reveal terminated employees paid in error, new hires missing from GL allocation, or manual paychecks circumventing normal systems.

Frequently asked questions

What is payroll reconciliation and why does it matter for month-end close?
Payroll reconciliation verifies that total payroll costs in your general ledger match what was actually paid through your payroll system — including gross pay, employer taxes, and benefits. Without it, the salary line on your P&L can carry errors for weeks before anyone notices.
Can Claude reconcile payroll across multiple pay frequencies in one pass?
Yes. If you run weekly payroll for hourly workers and monthly payroll for salaried staff, include both datasets and specify which cost centre or GL account each feeds into. Claude reconciles them separately and then produces a combined total for comparison against your GL.
Does Claude work with exports from ADP, Workday, or SAP?
Claude works with whatever data you paste into the prompt or upload as a file. Most payroll and ERP systems export to CSV or Excel. Clean the export to match the column layout in the prompt and Claude handles the rest.
How do I handle mid-month pay adjustments or off-cycle runs?
Include them as separate rows in your payroll extract with a Pay Type of "Off-cycle". The prompt instructs Claude to list them in a dedicated section at the bottom of the output so reviewers can find them without searching the main reconciliation table.
Can I use this to check individual employee records?
The main prompt is designed for cost-centre level reconciliation. Use the Employee-level check tab above for individual records — it matches by Employee ID and GL Account instead of by cost centre.
What causes most payroll-to-GL variances?
The three most common causes are: a payroll run posted to the wrong accounting period; employer tax or benefit costs coded to the wrong GL account; and a manual journal that adjusted gross pay without a matching payroll entry. The equal-and-opposite check in the prompt catches miscoding automatically.
