How to Build a 12 Month Rolling Forecast Excel Template with Claude AI

Paste your historical actuals and growth assumptions into Claude. It builds a 12-month rolling model where actuals replace forecasts as each month closes — always looking exactly 12 months ahead, driven by formulas not hard-coded numbers.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt:

[Paste your actuals here — at least 3 months, more is better]

- Seasonality index (optional): [e.g. Jan=0.85, Feb=0.90, ..., Dec=1.20]
- One-off items (optional): [e.g. Sep: £50,000 relocation cost in OpEx]

Current month (first open forecast month): [e.g. July 2025]

Requirements:
- Build a 12-month rolling model: actuals in the leftmost columns
  (blue header), forecast in the remaining columns (yellow header).
- Each month I will paste new actuals to replace the oldest forecast

- Forecast columns must use formulas referencing assumptions,
  NOT hard-coded values.

- Add one-off items as hard-coded overrides in the specified month.
- Include subtotal rows: Gross Profit, Operating Income, Net Income.
- Add a rolling 12-month total column on the far right.
- Do NOT invent figures or assumptions I haven't provided.
- Do NOT project beyond the 12-month horizon.
- Format as tab-separated table I can paste into Excel,
  with formulas written out (e.g. =B3*1.02).

Step-by-step workflow

Build a 12-month rolling forecast with Claude — 4 steps
1

Provide at least 3 months of closed actuals

Six to twelve months is ideal — it lets Claude see trends and seasonality in your data. The more history you provide, the better Claude can structure the actual-to-forecast transition and calibrate growth rates against real performance.

2

State specific growth and cost assumptions

"Revenue grows 2% per month" is actionable. "Revenue will increase" is not. Claude builds formulas from numbers — vague inputs produce vague models. Include COGS as a % of revenue, OpEx escalation rate, and any planned headcount changes with their hire months.

3

Put all assumptions on a separate tab

The prompt instructs Claude to reference an Assumptions tab for every forecast driver. Change one growth rate and every future month recalculates. Without this, you'd need to edit 36+ cells to update a single assumption across 12 months.

4

Update monthly using the Update with actuals prompt

At the end of each month, use the third tab prompt above. Paste the closed actuals, Claude gives you the replacement values and the new far-end forecast month. The whole update takes about 2 minutes and keeps the model rolling forward automatically.

Tip: Keep the rolling forecast alongside your static annual budget — not instead of it. Use the annual budget for board reporting and compensation benchmarks, the rolling forecast for operational decisions. Run the budget variance report against both to see which is tracking reality more closely.

Step-by-step workflow

Add a seasonality index to your forecast — 4 steps
1

Extract historical monthly data

Gather 2-3 years of historical revenue data to find a reliable pattern of monthly fluctuations.

2

Calculate the monthly weighting

Feed the data into Claude to convert the monthly splits into a base 1.0 index (e.g., if August is slow, it might be 0.8; if December is busy, it might be 1.5).

3

Isolate seasonality on an assumptions tab

Keep these modifiers separate from your main calculation sheet. This allows you to tweak seasonal expectations without breaking cell formulas.

4

Apply the index to rolling formulas

Multiply your base growth projections by the specific month's seasonality index to reflect realistic cash flow peaks and valleys.

Step-by-step workflow

Update model with closed actuals — 4 steps
1

Finalise month-end close

Ensure that all general ledger numbers are final and locked before importing them to overwrite your forecast data.

2

Run the Update prompt

Provide Claude with the finalized actuals to ensure formulas update precisely without manual column-dragging errors.

3

Paste actuals over old forecast

Replace the oldest forecast column (which represents the just-closed month) with the newly generated actuals values.

4

Append the new far-end month

Add Claude's new formula column at the end of the sheet to immediately extend the rolling horizon to a full 12 months again.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a rolling forecast and an annual budget?
An annual budget is set once and stays fixed for the fiscal year. A rolling forecast is updated every month: actuals replace the oldest forecast period, and a new period is added at the far end so the model always looks 12 months ahead. This keeps planning current with business conditions rather than locked to assumptions made 10 months ago.
How many months should a rolling forecast cover?
Twelve months is the most common horizon. Some organisations use 18 months to ensure the forecast always spans at least one full fiscal year. The prompt defaults to 12 but you can change the horizon in the requirements section.
Can Claude automatically pull actuals into the forecast each month?
Claude cannot connect to live data sources. It builds the Excel structure with clearly separated Actuals and Forecast columns. Each month, paste updated actuals into the Actuals section and the formulas recalculate the remaining forecast periods automatically. Use the Update with actuals prompt tab above to guide this process.
How do I handle seasonality in a rolling forecast?
Use the Add seasonality prompt tab above. Provide your historical monthly revenue split as percentages of annual revenue. Claude converts them into a seasonality index and applies it to the base forecast so Q4 projections reflect your historical uplift rather than a flat monthly average.
Does this replace a 3-statement financial model?
No. A rolling forecast focuses on the P&L and key operating metrics. A 3-statement model links the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. They serve different purposes and complement each other well.
