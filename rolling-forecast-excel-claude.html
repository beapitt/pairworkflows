How to Build a 12-Month Rolling Forecast in Excel with Claude AI

Paste your historical actuals and growth assumptions into Claude. It builds a 12-month rolling model where actuals replace forecasts as each month closes — always looking exactly 12 months ahead, driven by formulas not hard-coded numbers.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
I need a rolling 12-month forecast model in Excel.

HISTORICAL ACTUALS (columns: Line Item, then one column per closed month):
[Paste your actuals here — at least 3 months, preferably 6–12]

FORECAST ASSUMPTIONS:
- Revenue growth: [e.g. 2% month-over-month]
- COGS as % of revenue: [e.g. 40%]
- OpEx monthly escalation: [e.g. 0.5% per month]
- Seasonality index (optional): [e.g. Jan=0.85, Feb=0.90, ..., Dec=1.20]
- One-off items (optional): [e.g. Sep: £50,000 relocation cost in OpEx]

First open forecast month: [e.g. July 2025]

MODEL DEFINITION
- Keep historical actuals in an Actuals section to the left.
- Keep a separate ACTIVE FORECAST HORIZON containing exactly 12 future months,
  starting with the first open forecast month.
- When a month closes, move its final actual into the Actuals section, remove that
  month from the active forecast horizon, and append one new forecast month at the
  far right. The active forecast horizon must remain exactly 12 months.

Requirements:
- Use formulas referencing an Assumptions tab — do not hard-code recurring forecast drivers.
- Revenue formula must use one clearly defined base and apply growth only once.
- If seasonality is used, reference the month-specific index from the Assumptions tab.
- Add one-off items only in the specified month as explicit overrides/additions.
- Include subtotal rows: Gross Profit and Operating Income.
- Include Net Income only if I provide all required below-operating-income assumptions
  (for example interest and tax). Otherwise stop at Operating Income rather than inventing inputs.
- Add a Forecast 12M Total column that sums only the 12 active forecast months.
- Do NOT invent figures or assumptions I haven't provided.
- Format as tab-separated tables ready to paste into Excel, with formulas written out.

Paste your real actuals and assumptions. Works in Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini.

I need to add a seasonality index to my rolling forecast model.

BASE-YEAR ANNUAL REVENUE: [e.g. £600,000]

Historical monthly revenue split as % of annual revenue:
Jan: [%] Feb: [%] Mar: [%] Apr: [%] May: [%] Jun: [%]
Jul: [%] Aug: [%] Sep: [%] Oct: [%] Nov: [%] Dec: [%]

Forecast growth assumption:
[e.g. 2% month-over-month, or leave blank if growth is already built into my base forecast]

Requirements:
- First verify that the 12 monthly shares total approximately 100%.
- Convert each monthly share into a normalized seasonality index:
  SeasonIndex = MonthlyShare / (1/12) = MonthlyShare × 12
  so the average index across the 12 months is 1.0.
- Define BaseMonthlyRevenue = BaseYearAnnualRevenue / 12.
- If growth is NOT already embedded elsewhere, use:
  Forecast = BaseMonthlyRevenue × SeasonIndex × (1 + GrowthRate)^n
  where n = 0 for the first forecast month, 1 for the next month, etc.
- If my existing base forecast already includes growth, do NOT multiply by growth again;
  apply only the seasonality adjustment.
- Put the seasonality index on a separate Assumptions tab.
- Write the exact Excel formula for each forecast revenue cell.
- Do not invent missing monthly shares or growth assumptions.

Build a rolling forecast step by step

Build a 12-month rolling forecast with Claude — 4 steps
1

Provide at least 3 months of closed actuals

Six to twelve months is ideal — it lets Claude see trends and seasonality in your data. The more history you provide, the better Claude can structure the actual-to-forecast transition and calibrate growth rates against real performance.

2

State specific growth and cost assumptions

"Revenue grows 2% per month" is actionable. "Revenue will increase" is not. Claude builds formulas from numbers — vague inputs produce vague models. Include COGS as a % of revenue, OpEx escalation rate, and any planned headcount changes with their hire months.

3

Put all assumptions on a separate tab

The prompt instructs Claude to reference an Assumptions tab for every forecast driver. Change one growth rate and every future month recalculates. Without this, you'd need to edit 36+ cells to update a single assumption across 12 months.

4

Update monthly using the Update with actuals prompt

At the end of each month, use the third tab prompt above. Paste the closed actuals; Claude can identify the cells to move into history and provide the new far-end forecast formulas while keeping the active horizon at 12 future months.

Tip: Keep the rolling forecast alongside your static annual budget — not instead of it. Use the annual budget for board reporting and compensation benchmarks, the rolling forecast for operational decisions. Run the budget variance report against both to see which is tracking reality more closely.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a rolling forecast and an annual budget?
An annual budget is set once and stays fixed for the fiscal year. A rolling forecast is updated every month: the closed month moves into historical actuals, that month leaves the active forecast horizon, and a new period is added at the far end so the model continues to look 12 months ahead. This keeps planning current with business conditions rather than locked to assumptions made 10 months ago.
How many months should a rolling forecast cover?
Twelve months is the most common horizon. Some organisations use 18 months to ensure the forecast always spans at least one full fiscal year. The prompt defaults to 12 but you can change the horizon in the requirements section.
Can Claude automatically pull actuals into the forecast each month?
Claude cannot connect to live data sources. It builds the Excel structure with clearly separated Actuals and Forecast columns. Each month, paste the closed month into the Actuals section, roll the active forecast forward by one month, and append a new far-end forecast period using the same linked assumptions. Use the Update with actuals prompt tab above to guide this process.
How do I handle seasonality in a rolling forecast?
Use the Add seasonality prompt tab above. Provide your historical monthly revenue split as percentages of annual revenue. Claude converts them into a seasonality index and applies it to the base forecast so Q4 projections reflect your historical uplift rather than a flat monthly average.
Does this replace a 3-statement financial model?
No. A rolling forecast focuses on the P&L and key operating metrics. A 3-statement model links the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. They serve different purposes and complement each other well.
