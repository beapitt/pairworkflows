Claude Power User Habits for Faster, Sharper Sessions

Five small habits separate people who get outstanding results from Claude from everyone else — none takes more than 30 seconds. Context summaries, prompt saving, lean file formats, saved preferences, and a validation check before you hit send.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
Summarise this conversation into a context summary I can paste into a new chat.

Include:
- The goal we were working on
- The key decisions made
- The context I gave you
- Where we left off

Keep it under 200 words. Write it so a new Claude conversation could pick up
exactly where this one left off, with no other information.

Run this at the end of any productive session, before you close the tab. Paste into Claude.

Based on our conversation, write me the single best prompt I could use to get
the same quality output from scratch, in a new chat, with no prior context.

Make it complete and self-contained — include the role, the context, the task,
and the format I ended up wanting. I want to reuse this next time I need the
same kind of output.

Your best prompts usually emerge mid-conversation, not upfront. This captures them before they disappear.

Before I share your last response with [a colleague / a client / my manager],
review it as if you were the person receiving it, not the person who asked
for it.

Check:
- Every number and date — are they consistent with what I gave you?
- Every name and title — correct and spelled right?
- Every claim — does it follow from the source material, or did you infer it?

List anything uncertain or unverifiable separately. Do not soften this check.

A confident-sounding output is not automatically a correct one. Run this before anything gets sent onward.

Step-by-step workflow

Build the 5 power user habits — 5 steps
1

Save a context summary at the end of every session

Before you close a productive chat, ask Claude to distil it into a reusable summary: goal, decisions, context, and where you left off. Paste it at the start of your next conversation instead of rebuilding from scratch.

2

Reverse-engineer your best prompts

After any session that produced great results, ask Claude to write the single best prompt that would reproduce the same quality from scratch. Save it — your best prompts emerge in conversation, not upfront.

3

Choose lean file formats

Upload .txt or .md instead of PDF or Word whenever you can. Plain text uses fewer tokens than formatted documents, leaving more of your usage limit for the response itself.

4

Set your preferences once in Settings

Under your profile icon → Settings → Preferences, add your role, preferred response style, and any standing rules. Claude applies them automatically — no need to re-type instructions at the start of every chat.

5

Validate before you share anything

Claude sounds confident even when it is wrong. Read any output that will be shared or acted on as the decision-maker, not the person who asked for it — check numbers, names, and claims before it goes anywhere.

Compound effect: none of these habits takes more than 30 seconds to apply on their own. Used together and consistently, they are what separates professionals who get outstanding results from Claude from those who get average ones.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What is a context summary and why does it matter?
A context summary is a short handover note — the goal, key decisions, context given, and where you left off — that you paste into a new chat so Claude can continue without you rebuilding everything from scratch. It is not a transcript, just the minimum Claude needs.
How do I save a good prompt after a great Claude session?
Ask Claude to reverse-engineer the single best prompt that would produce the same quality output from scratch, with no prior context. Save that prompt and reuse it — your best prompts usually emerge mid-conversation, not upfront.
Why does file format affect my Claude usage limit?
Plain text files like .txt or .md use significantly fewer tokens than PDFs or Word documents because Claude only processes the words, not formatting, headers, footers, and metadata. Lighter files leave more of your usage limit for the response itself.
What is Settings Memory in Claude?
Claude's Settings let you set your role, preferred tone, and standing formatting rules once, under Profile → Settings → Preferences. Claude applies them automatically to every conversation, so you stop re-typing the same instructions each time you open a chat.
Why should I validate Claude's output before sharing it?
Claude can sound confident even when it is wrong. Before sharing any output with a colleague, client, or manager, read it as the decision-maker — check numbers, names, and claims. A confident-sounding output is not automatically a correct one.
