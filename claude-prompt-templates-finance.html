Step-by-step workflow

Adapt these templates to your Finance team — 5 steps
1

Specify your currency and reporting standard

Add a line after the role framing: All figures are in [GBP/USD/EUR]. Reporting standard: [IFRS/UK GAAP/US GAAP]. This stops Claude assuming currency or applying the wrong accounting terminology for your jurisdiction.

2

Define your fiscal year and period naming

Add: Our fiscal year runs [April to March]. Use [FY26/Q2 FY26] format. Period labels in the output then match your internal reporting exactly, instead of drifting to calendar-year assumptions.

3

State the audience explicitly

A CFO board summary and a departmental update need different vocabulary. Tell Claude who is reading — "non-Finance board audience" versus "Finance Director" — and the tone shifts without you rewriting the structure.

4

Add a verification flag for board-facing output

Append: Flag any figure you cannot verify from the data provided with [NEEDS DATA VERIFICATION]. This turns Claude into a first-draft assistant, not a source of unchecked numbers.

5

Save your adapted templates as your team standard

Once a template reflects your currency, fiscal calendar, and board terminology, store it centrally. Every future draft starts from your settings instead of a generic prompt.

Never skip human review: Claude can draft, structure, and frame financial commentary — but every figure, percentage, and forward-looking statement must be verified by a qualified Finance professional before it reaches a board, an auditor, or a regulator.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What makes Finance prompting different from other departments?
Three things: numerical precision, compliance-aware language, and audience calibration. Finance outputs reference exact figures and periods, often carry regulatory weight, and need different phrasing for a board summary than for an operational update. Generic prompts skip all three.
What should I add to stop Claude guessing at currency or accounting standard?
Add a line after the role framing: "All figures are in [GBP/USD/EUR]. Reporting standard: [IFRS/UK GAAP/US GAAP]." This stops Claude applying the wrong terminology or assuming a currency you didn't specify.
When should I use a verification flag like [NEEDS DATA VERIFICATION]?
On any output headed for a board, auditor, or regulator. Ask Claude to flag any figure it cannot verify from the data you provided, so nothing gets treated as confirmed when it's actually an inference.
What is Claude's role in high-stakes Finance communications?
First-draft assistant, not final-output generator. Claude can draft, structure, and frame financial commentary, but every figure, percentage, and forward-looking statement needs review by a qualified Finance professional before it reaches a board, auditor, or regulator.
Can I reuse these templates across different reporting periods?
Yes — that's the point of a template. Once you've added your currency, fiscal year format, and board terminology preferences, the same structure works every month or quarter. You just swap the bracketed figures.
