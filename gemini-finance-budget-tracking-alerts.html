Budget Tracking and Alerts with Gemini AI in Google Sheets

A budget overspend is usually sitting in the spreadsheet well before month-end — the number is there, but nobody has flagged it yet. Gemini reviews your budget tracking sheet against a fair run-rate, flags the lines trending over, and drafts the alert message so budget holders hear about it early, not after the fact.

My budget review keeps coming back with nothing flagged, even though
I know a department is overspending.

Here is my budget tracking table:
[Paste: Department, Annual Budget, Spend to Date, Months Elapsed, Budget Holder]

I do not currently have an Expected Spend to Date column — variance is
being compared against the full annual budget instead of a fair run-rate.

Please:
1. Calculate Expected Spend to Date for each line
   (Annual Budget ÷ 12 × Months Elapsed)
2. Recalculate variance against that expected figure, not the annual total
3. Re-run the flagging check and list any lines now over the [10%] threshold

A department that looks fine against the annual total can already be badly over its fair run-rate. This fixes the comparison, not just the flagging.

Keep each message short enough to read in one glance — a budget holder who ignores a long message will also ignore the next one.

Step-by-step workflow

Set up Gemini for budget tracking and alerts — 5 steps
1

Build a tracking sheet with the right columns

At minimum: Department, Annual Budget, Spend to Date, Months Elapsed, Expected Spend to Date, Variance, Budget Holder. Missing any of these breaks the review — the Expected Spend to Date and Budget Holder columns are the two most often left out.

2

Update spend-to-date figures on a fixed schedule

Weekly or monthly, whichever fits your close cycle. Gemini only sees what you share, so stale figures produce a stale review — this is not something it can chase up on its own.

3

Agree a variance threshold before you start

Decide what counts as Over budget versus Watch (e.g. >10% and 5-10%) so the flagging is consistent from one review to the next, rather than judged case by case.

4

Run the flagging prompt and check for zero flags too

A clean result is a real result. If nothing is flagged, that's useful information on its own — don't lower the threshold just to manufacture a finding.

5

Draft and send the alert message the same day

Use the alert prompt to turn each flagged line into a short message for the budget holder while the figures are still current, not a week later.

No real-time monitoring: Gemini does not run in the background and cannot watch your spreadsheet on its own. For this to work as an early-warning system, sharing updated figures has to become a regular habit — not a one-off review.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What role does Gemini play in budget tracking?
Gemini does not monitor your spreadsheet automatically or send alerts on its own. You bring it the current figures from your budget tracking sheet, and it helps you spot the lines that are over or trending over budget and draft the message to send to the budget holder.
Why is an Expected Spend to Date column useful?
It gives Gemini a fair run-rate to compare against, based on how many months of the year have elapsed. Comparing spend to date directly against the full annual budget makes every line look fine until month 12 — comparing against the expected spend to date catches overspend months earlier.
What happens if no budget lines exceed the threshold?
Gemini should say clearly that no lines exceed the threshold rather than lowering the bar until something qualifies. If nothing is flagged, that is a useful result on its own — it means the current variance threshold has not been breached this period.
Does Gemini monitor my spreadsheet automatically?
No. Gemini does not run in the background or send alerts on its own. It only reviews the figures you share with it in a given session, so for this to work as an early-warning system, sharing updated figures needs to become a regular habit — weekly or monthly, not once a year.
What columns should my budget tracking sheet include?
At minimum: Department, Annual Budget, Spend to Date, Months Elapsed, Expected Spend to Date, Variance, and Budget Holder. The Expected Spend to Date and Budget Holder columns are the two most commonly missing, and both are essential — one for accurate flagging, the other for knowing who to alert.
