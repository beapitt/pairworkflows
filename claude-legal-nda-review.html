Claude AI for NDA Review

NDAs land in your inbox every week, and reading each one carefully enough to spot a one-sided clause takes time you rarely have. Paste the NDA into Claude and flag the key issues before your attorney spends time on the initial read.

Choose your situation — copy the prompt: 
Review the following NDA and flag any clauses that appear one-sided, unusual or that may be missing standard protections: [Insert NDA Text].

Context: I am the [disclosing / receiving / mutual] party. The governing law is [Jurisdiction]. The purpose of the NDA is [Insert Brief Description].

Flag issues under these headings: Definition of Confidential Information, Duration and Survival, Obligations on Each Party, Carve-outs and Exceptions, Return or Destruction of Information, and Any Other Unusual Clauses.

Crucial instruction: Flag issues based on the text provided. Do not invent clauses that are missing unless their absence is notable for a standard NDA. Do not provide legal advice on whether to sign.

State your position clearly — disclosing vs. receiving changes what should be flagged. Paste into Claude.

Summarise the following NDA in plain English for a non-lawyer audience: [Insert NDA Text].

Cover: what each party must keep confidential, for how long, what is excluded from confidentiality, and what happens at the end of the agreement.

Crucial instruction: Summarise only what the NDA actually says. Do not add legal interpretations, recommend changes or suggest whether the NDA is favourable or not.

Good for briefing business stakeholders who need to understand the NDA quickly.

Review the following NDA and assess whether the obligations are balanced between both parties: [Insert NDA Text].

I am the [disclosing / receiving / mutual] party.

For each obligation (confidentiality duty, return of information, remedies, liability), state which party it favours and by how much. Note any obligations that apply to only one party when a mutual NDA would typically apply them to both.

Crucial instruction: Base the assessment strictly on the text provided. Flag imbalance — do not recommend whether to accept it.

Useful when you suspect a "mutual" NDA is not actually mutual in practice.

Step-by-step workflow

Review an NDA with Claude — 5 steps
1

Identify your position and priorities

Before reviewing, clarify whether you are the disclosing party, the receiving party or both — and what information you most need to protect.

2

Paste the NDA and request a risk review

Ask Claude to review the NDA and flag clauses that are one-sided, unusual or missing, based on your position in the agreement.

3

Request a plain English summary

Ask Claude to summarise the key obligations in plain language so the relevant business stakeholders can understand what they are signing.

4

Brief your attorney with Claude's findings

Share Claude's flagged issues with your attorney — this focuses the legal review on the real risks and saves time on the initial read.

5

Negotiate or approve based on attorney advice

Use the attorney's assessment — not Claude's — to decide which clauses to push back on and which to accept.

Never sign on Claude's findings alone: Claude's NDA review is a preliminary screening tool, not legal advice. An attorney must review the document before execution, particularly for NDAs involving sensitive IP, large commercial relationships or unusual governing law clauses.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What context should I always give Claude when reviewing an NDA?
Whether you are the disclosing party, receiving party or both, and the governing jurisdiction. NDA standards vary significantly by jurisdiction and by which side of the agreement you're on.
What is Claude's NDA review designed to do?
Provide a preliminary screening that flags issues for attorney review — not a substitute for legal advice. It focuses the attorney's time on the real risks instead of the initial read-through.
Why do NDA standards vary so much between jurisdictions?
What is unusual in one jurisdiction may be entirely standard in another — attorney confirmation is always needed to know whether a flagged clause is actually a problem in your context.
Who decides whether to sign an NDA that Claude has reviewed?
An attorney, based on their own assessment — not Claude's findings alone. Which clauses to push back on and whether to sign is always a decision based on qualified legal advice.
Can Claude tell me if an NDA's obligations are one-sided?
Yes — ask it to assess reciprocity based on your position (disclosing, receiving, or mutual). It will flag clauses that appear to favour one party, but a qualified attorney should confirm whether that imbalance is actually a problem.
