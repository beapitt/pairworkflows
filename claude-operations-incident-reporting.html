Claude AI for Incident Reporting

A system outage or safety event demands a report that's fast and factually airtight. Give Claude your raw notes and it structures a professional draft with a timeline, impact summary and corrective actions — you verify every fact before it goes anywhere.

Crucial instruction: Base every section strictly on the facts provided. Do not infer causes, add context or include details not present in the notes.

Write your notes down as close to the event as possible. Paste into Claude.

Keep the tone [Insert Tone, e.g. factual and calm / reassuring / direct].

Step-by-step workflow

Draft an incident report with Claude — 5 steps
1

Record the facts immediately

Write down what happened, when, where, who was involved and what actions were taken — as close to the event as possible, while details are fresh.

2

Identify the required report format

Check whether your organisation or regulator requires a specific report structure, and note any mandatory sections.

3

Prepare your source data

If working from a spreadsheet, make sure the data is clean: consistent date formats, no duplicate rows, and complete entries.

4

Ask Claude to draft the report

Paste your notes or prepared data and ask Claude to produce a structured incident report with a timeline, impact summary and corrective actions section.

5

Verify every fact and approve

Read the draft against your original notes, confirm accuracy, and have the report reviewed by the appropriate manager or safety officer before submitting.

Legal implications: Incident reports for safety events, regulatory incidents or insurance claims may have legal implications. Never submit a report that contains information you cannot verify — and always follow your organisation's incident reporting procedures.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What does Claude need to draft an incident report?
The facts of the incident — what happened, when, who was involved and what actions were taken. Claude structures and drafts from facts you provide; it does not know what actually happened.
What is the most important check before submitting a Claude-drafted incident report?
Verifying that every date, time, name and action in the draft matches your original notes exactly. Claude may reorder or reword events to improve clarity, so the sequence must always be checked.
Can Claude infer the cause of an incident from my notes?
It shouldn't — instruct it not to. Every incident report prompt should specify that Claude bases every section strictly on the facts provided and does not infer causes or add context not present in the notes.
Why does cleaning spreadsheet data matter before drafting a report from it?
Because duplicates, inconsistent date formats or blank rows in an incident log can produce an inaccurate or incomplete report. Clean the data first — consistent formats, no duplicate rows, complete entries.
Do incident reports for safety or regulatory events need extra care?
Yes. Incident reports for safety events, regulatory incidents or insurance claims may have legal implications. Never submit a report containing information you cannot verify, and always follow your organisation's incident reporting procedures.
