Step-by-step workflow

Verify a Claude output before you trust it — 5 steps
1

Ask Claude to flag what it's uncertain about

Before you read the output as finished, send a follow-up asking Claude to flag any claims it isn't certain about. It won't catch everything — but it eliminates the most obvious risks in seconds.

2

Check every specific number against a source

Any percentage, figure, or statistic needs a primary source. A quick search takes 30 seconds and prevents a credibility problem in a board presentation or client document.

3

Search for every citation before you use it

If Claude mentions a report, study, or article — search for it before including it. If it doesn't exist, remove it. A fabricated citation in an external document is a serious professional risk.

4

Read the output as the recipient, not the author

After verifying the facts, read the output as if you're the manager, client, or regulator receiving it. Does anything read as surprising, overconfident, or unusually specific? That's your final signal to check.

5

Apply the high-stakes rule

If the output will be shared with a client, presented to leadership, submitted to a regulator, or published externally — run the verification prompt first, then check every flagged item before you send it.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
Why does Claude sound confident even when it's wrong?
Claude does not know when it doesn't know something. It generates a plausible-sounding answer even when working beyond what it actually knows — the output reads clearly and confidently whether or not it is correct. This is a fundamental characteristic of how large language models work, not a bug.
What are the three types of errors Claude can make?
Hallucination — inventing a fact, statistic, name, or source that doesn't exist. Outdated information — Claude's training data has a cutoff, so laws, prices, and personnel may have changed. Misinterpretation — Claude understood the question differently from how you meant it, so the answer is technically correct but misses the point.
Which Claude outputs carry the highest risk of being wrong?
Specific statistics or percentages, named citations and references, and legal or regulatory detail carry the highest risk. Named individuals and quotes carry medium risk. General concepts, explanations, and formatting carry low risk.
What's the fastest way to verify a high-stakes Claude output?
Ask Claude to flag what it's uncertain about. Send a follow-up like: "In your previous response, flag any claims you are not certain about and tell me what I should verify." It won't catch everything, but it eliminates the most obvious risks in seconds and tells you exactly where to focus your manual check.
When should I run a full verification pass before using Claude's output?
If the output will be shared with a client, presented to leadership, submitted to a regulator, or published externally, run the verification prompt first and check every flagged item before you send it.
