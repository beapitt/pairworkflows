Step-by-step workflow

Build a QBR narrative with Claude — 5 steps
1

Gather and clean the data

Pull raw numbers from your dashboard or spreadsheet. Make sure the data is clean — no duplicate rows, consistent date formats, no blank cells that could confuse the narrative.

2

Define the goal for this meeting

Decide upfront whether this QBR is an upsell pitch or an at-risk renewal defense. The narrative Claude builds should point toward that specific outcome.

3

Paste data, history, and goal into Claude

Drop your usage data, account history, and meeting goal into the prompt. Vague inputs produce a vague story.

4

Generate the slide-by-slide narrative

Ask Claude for a storyline connecting each data point to the goal — title, 3 bullets, talking script per slide.

5

Layer in context and build the deck

Add the internal politics or recent client news Claude couldn't know, then drop the finalized text into your actual slide template.

Validate before presenting: QBR narratives must always be checked against the client's actual contract terms and agreed success metrics. Never present an AI-generated claim as a contractual fact or a guaranteed ROI.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
What is Claude's primary role when preparing a QBR?
Claude acts as a strategic ghostwriter. You give it raw usage metrics and account history, and it connects the isolated numbers to the client's business goals to build a cohesive narrative, rather than just summarizing the data.
What is a direct limitation of using Claude for QBRs?
Claude can't generate the actual PowerPoint or Google Slides file. It only provides the text, slide titles, bullet points, and the talking script — you still have to build the deck yourself.
What must I do after Claude generates the narrative and before building slides?
Add your own executive context — internal politics, a recent leadership change, or client news that Claude has no way of knowing. The AI-generated narrative is a first draft, not the final word.
What is the most critical check before presenting a Claude-written QBR narrative?
Validate every claim against the client's actual contract terms and agreed success metrics. Never present an AI-generated statement as a contractual fact or a guaranteed ROI figure.
How does Claude turn raw usage data into a story?
It connects each metric to the client's stated business goal and explains the "so what" behind the number — for example, translating a 38% rise in exports into a claim about cutting the ops team's manual reporting time in half.
