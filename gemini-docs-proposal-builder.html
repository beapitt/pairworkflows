How to Write a Business Proposal with Gemini AI in Google Docs

Draft each section of a business proposal directly in Google Docs using Gemini. Paste your discovery call notes, specify the client's challenge and budget range, and get a structured draft — executive summary, solution, pricing narrative, and timeline — in under 30 minutes.

Choose your section — copy the prompt: 
Act as an enterprise sales writer. Draft the Executive Summary
section of a business proposal using these inputs:

CLIENT: [Client company name]
THEIR CHALLENGE: [The specific problem they described in the
 discovery call — use their exact words where possible]
OUR SOLUTION: [One sentence on what you are proposing]
KEY OUTCOME: [The measurable result they care about most —
 e.g. "reduce onboarding time by 40%"]
SIMILAR RESULT: [A comparable result you achieved for another
 client — e.g. "consistent with the result we achieved for
 [Company X] in Q3 2025"]
BUDGET RANGE: [e.g. £15,000–£20,000]

Requirements:
- Keep it under 150 words — decision-makers often read
  only this section before deciding to continue.
- Name the client's specific challenge in the first sentence.
- Include the key outcome and the comparable result.
- End with a one-sentence statement of why now.
- Direct, professional tone. No phrases like "world-class",
  "synergy", or "seamlessly".
- Do NOT invent figures, outcomes, or client details.

In Google Docs: place cursor where you want the text → click the Gemini icon → paste this prompt. Works in Gemini or Claude too.

Step-by-step workflow

Write a business proposal with Gemini — 5 steps
1

Prepare your inputs before opening Google Docs

The quality of the draft depends entirely on what you put in. Before opening Docs, write down: the client's exact challenge in their words, the specific outcome they mentioned, one comparable result you've achieved, your real timeline broken into phases, and your actual pricing. With these ready, the prompts take 5 minutes to run.

2

Use "Help me write" for each section separately

Don't ask Gemini to write the whole proposal in one prompt — section-by-section produces far better output. In Google Docs, place your cursor at the start of each section, click the Gemini pencil icon, and paste the relevant prompt. The Gemini side panel is context-aware and can reference earlier sections you've already drafted.

3

Draft the executive summary last, not first

The executive summary is the most-read section — many decision-makers read only this before deciding to continue. Write it last, once you have the solution, timeline, and pricing clear. Use the Executive summary prompt above and include your real comparable result: "consistent with results we achieved for [Company] in [Quarter]".

4

Add one discovery-call sentence to every section

After Gemini drafts each section, add one sentence that references something specific the client said on your call. That sentence is what clients notice most — it signals you actually listened. AI cannot write this sentence. You write it.

5

Read it aloud before sending

Read the whole proposal aloud. Any sentence that feels unnatural to say is a sentence the client will notice feels unnatural to read. Use the Refine tone prompt to fix stiff or formulaic paragraphs. Check every number and date against your notes before exporting as PDF.

Never let Gemini invent pricing or timelines. These are contractual commitments. If you leave pricing blank in the prompt, Gemini will fill in plausible-sounding figures that may be completely wrong. Always supply your real numbers and verify them in the final document before sending.

Frequently asked questions

What sections should a business proposal include?
Five sections: an Executive Summary (under 150 words — the most read), a Problem Statement naming the client's specific challenge, a Solution section explaining your approach in phases, a Pricing and Timeline table with specific milestones, and a Why Us section. Gemini drafts each from your raw notes — you supply the specific details.
How does Gemini in Google Docs work for proposal writing?
Two ways: the Help me write button (place your cursor, click the pencil icon, type your prompt) and the side panel (context-aware — it knows what's in your document and can reference specific sections). As of 2026, Gemini is included in all paid Google Workspace plans at no extra cost.
What makes an AI-generated proposal feel generic?
Three things: a prompt that doesn't include the client's name and specific pain point; no customization after the draft (skipping the one sentence from your discovery call); and accepting the first draft without reading it aloud to check if it sounds like a real person wrote it.
Can Gemini write the pricing section?
Gemini can format the pricing table and write the explanatory narrative around your numbers — but you must supply the actual figures. Never let AI invent pricing or timelines. Use the Solution & timeline prompt above and fill in your real numbers before running it.
Should I send an AI-generated proposal without editing it?
Never. Every proposal needs a manual review for: accuracy of all metrics and dates, client name spelled correctly throughout, no pricing commitments the AI invented, and at least one sentence that references something specific from your discovery call. That sentence is often what clients notice most.
