Step-by-step workflow

Adapt these templates to your brand — 5 steps
1

Define your brand voice in three words

Add to every template: Brand voice: [word 1], [word 2], [word 3]. Example: confident, human, jargon-free. Three words give Claude enough direction to calibrate tone without over-constraining the output. Avoid vague descriptors like "professional" alone.

2

Add your banned words and phrases

Every Marketing team has phrases they're tired of seeing. Add them explicitly: Never use: synergy, game-changer, cutting-edge, excited to announce, thrilled to share. This single addition eliminates the most common reason teams reject AI-generated content.

3

Specify your audience with a job-to-be-done

Instead of just a job title, add what that person is trying to achieve: Audience: HR Directors at mid-market UK companies trying to reduce turnover without increasing headcount costs. This gives Claude a real problem to write toward, not just a demographic label.

4

Link every asset to your campaign narrative

Add one sentence of context: This post is part of our Q3 campaign focused on [theme]. The overarching message is [key message]. This is the difference between a campaign and a collection of random posts.

5

Save your adapted templates for the whole team

Once customised, save the versions in a shared doc your Marketing team can reuse. Consistent prompting ensures consistent brand voice across every team member — not just the most experienced copywriter.

Always review before publishing. Claude can draft, structure, and refine Marketing content — but brand accuracy, factual claims, and legal sign-off must always come from a human. Use it to eliminate the blank page problem, not to replace the review step.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
Why do generic Claude prompts often fail for Marketing content?
Generic prompts produce content with no brand voice, no audience specificity, and no campaign context. In Marketing, that's not just a quality problem — a LinkedIn post that sounds like every other brand or an email that speaks to nobody in particular fails to convert, no matter how well-written the sentences are.
What is included in every Marketing prompt template in this library?
Role framing, brand voice descriptors, audience definition, and format constraints — the elements that turn a generic AI output into on-brand content your team can actually publish, without heavy manual editing.
How can I stop Claude from using overused marketing phrases like "excited to announce"?
Add an explicit list of banned phrases directly inside the prompt — e.g. "Never use: synergy, game-changer, cutting-edge, excited to announce, thrilled to share." Claude will actively avoid them.
What is the recommended final step before publishing any Marketing content drafted by Claude?
Have a human review it. Claude can draft, structure, and refine Marketing content, but brand accuracy, factual claims, and legal sign-off must always come from a person before anything is published.
How quickly can I adapt a generic Marketing template to my brand voice?
In under two minutes. Add three brand voice descriptors, ban your overused phrases, frame your audience with a job-to-be-done, and link the asset to your campaign narrative — every future use already reflects your brand.
