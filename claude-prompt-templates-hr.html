Claude AI Prompt Templates for HR Professionals

HR deals with some of the most sensitive communications in any organisation. These templates build in role framing, tone constraints, compliance awareness, and length control — the four elements that turn a generic AI output into something you can actually send.

Act as an HR Business Partner writing for [Company Name], a [Company

position. The role reports to [Manager Title]. Key responsibilities:
[list 3-4 bullet points]. Required skills: [list 3-4 skills]. Tone:
professional but human — we want to attract candidates, not intimidate
them. Format: short intro paragraph, responsibilities, requirements, one
closing sentence about culture. Maximum 300 words.

Replace the bracketed sections with your specifics. Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini.

Act as an experienced HR Manager preparing written performance review
feedback. The employee is [Name], role: [Job Title]. Overall performance
this period: [Strong/Meets expectations/Needs improvement]. Key
achievement: [one sentence]. Key development area: [one sentence]. Write
balanced, constructive feedback in 3 short paragraphs: strengths,
development area, and forward-looking goal. Tone: direct but supportive.
No generic phrases like "team player" or "hard worker." Maximum 200
words.

Banning generic phrases in the prompt itself produces feedback that actually reads as specific to the employee.

Act as a senior HR Manager at [Company Name]. Draft a rejection email for
[Candidate Name], an internal candidate who applied for [Role Title].
They were not selected. Tone: empathetic, respectful, forward-looking.
Acknowledge their effort, give one specific growth area, and encourage
future applications. Do not use corporate jargon. Maximum 150 words.

Works for both internal and external candidates — just adjust the "encourage future applications" line if it's an external hire.

Step-by-step workflow

Adapt these templates to your company — 5 steps
1

Add your company tone descriptor

Add a line like: Our company tone is [formal and professional / conversational and direct / warm and inclusive]. This single addition shifts the entire register without changing the structure.

2

Reference your specific policies

For disciplinary or compliance-sensitive communications, add: This must align with our [UK employment law / GDPR / company handbook] requirements. Claude will flag language that may need legal review.

3

Ban your company's overused phrases

Add them explicitly: Do not use the following phrases: [synergy, going forward, circle back, touch base, passionate about]. Claude will actively avoid them.

4

Store your adapted version as your master template

Once customised for your company, save it as your standard version. Every future use already reflects your tone, policy, and language preferences.

5

Always review before sending

Every HR communication — especially disciplinary and rejection messages — must be reviewed by a human before it reaches the recipient.

Claude is a drafting assistant, not a signatory. Use it to eliminate the blank page problem, not the review step.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions
Why do generic Claude prompts often fail for HR communications?
They lack empathy constraints, compliance awareness, and role-specific framing. In HR, that's not just a quality problem — a rejection email that sounds like a press release or a performance review that reads like a legal contract is a compliance and reputational risk.
What is included in every HR prompt template in this course?
Role framing, tone constraints, compliance awareness, and length control — the four elements that turn a generic output into something you can actually send, without heavy manual editing.
How can I stop Claude from using phrases my company considers overused or inappropriate?
Add an explicit list of banned phrases directly inside the prompt — e.g. "Do not use the following phrases: synergy, going forward, circle back." Claude will actively avoid them.
What is the recommended final step before sending any HR communication drafted by Claude?
Have a human review it. Claude is a drafting assistant, not a signatory — every HR communication, especially disciplinary and rejection messages, must be reviewed by a human before it reaches the recipient.
How quickly can I adapt a generic HR template to my company's voice?
In under two minutes. Add a tone descriptor, reference your specific policies, ban your overused phrases, and save the result as your master template — every future use already reflects your company's voice.
